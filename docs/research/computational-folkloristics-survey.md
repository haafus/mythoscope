# Computational Folkloristics & Computational Mythology: A Survey for Mythoscope

> Survey compiled July 2026. Intended location: `docs/research/computational-folkloristics-survey.md`

## TL;DR
- The field splits into two eras: a **classical era (roughly 2008–2018)** built on digitizing and formalizing the Aarne-Thompson-Uther (ATU) tale-type index and the Thompson Motif Index (TMI), topic-model motif detection (Karsdorp, Meder), geo-semantic exploration (Tangherlini's WitchHunter), phylogenetics of tales/myths (Tehrani, d'Huy, Berezkin) and character-network analysis (Mac Carron & Kenna); and a **modern era (2018–2026)** dominated by transformer embeddings, BERTopic, and LLM-based motif/type annotation.
- For an embedding-based motif search tool, the most directly useful assets are **sentence-transformer/LaBSE embeddings + HDBSCAN/UMAP clustering** (validated by Tangherlini & Chen 2024 and Arčon et al. 2025), the **ATU/TMI indices as an ontology backbone** (Declerck; MOMFER's WordNet expansion), and a handful of **open corpora** (FairytaleQA, the Annotated Folktales "trilogy" corpus, MFTD, Berezkin's catalogue).
- The honest state of the art: motif detection is **still unsolved** — motifs are "formally fluid," gold-standard training data is scarce, and even 2024–2025 LLM approaches are frequently matched by simple SVM/TF-IDF baselines. Mythoscope should treat embeddings as a *retrieval and candidate-generation* layer over a curated index, not as a reliable end-to-end motif classifier.

## Key Findings
1. **The indices are the substrate.** Almost all computational folkloristics is scaffolded on two mid-20th-century reference works. The ATU tale-type index (Uther 2004, *The Types of International Folktales*, FF Communications 284–286, three volumes of 619 + 536 + 285 pp.) is the third iteration and fifth revision of the type index first published by Antti Aarne in 1910; Uther added "more than two hundred and fifty new types" over the 1961 Aarne-Thompson index and, per da Silva & Tehrani, it "indexes over 2,000 fairy tales distributed among more than 200 societies." Stith Thompson's *Motif-Index of Folk-Literature* (1955–58) codes tens of thousands of narrative elements alphanumerically (classes A–Z). Yuri Berezkin's *Analytical Catalogue of World Mythology and Folklore* is a third, mythology-oriented scheme with a different (episode/image) definition of "motif."
2. **Topic models were the first workhorse for motif induction** (Karsdorp & van den Bosch 2013). These have now been largely superseded by **BERTopic** (embeddings + UMAP + HDBSCAN + class-based TF-IDF), used by Tangherlini & Chen (2024) on Hans Christian Andersen.
3. **Phylogenetic/evolutionary methods** treat tales as evolving lineages, coding presence/absence of tale types or motifs across cultures and running Bayesian/parsimony phylogenetics (da Silva & Tehrani 2016; d'Huy on Cosmic Hunt & Polyphemus; Thuillard/d'Huy/Le Quellec/Berezkin 2018).
4. **Network analysis** operates at two levels: character/social networks within a narrative (Mac Carron & Kenna 2012; the Odyssey network 2018) and motif/tale-type networks across a corpus (Abello, Broadwell & Tangherlini's "folklore hairball").
5. **Propp function extraction** is the hardest structural task; Finlayson's Analogical Story Merging (2012/2016) learned a substantive portion of Propp's morphology from deeply annotated text, but 2024 LLM attempts to tag Propp functions reported largely *negative* results.
6. **LLM/embedding motif work (2024–2026)** shows promise for cross-lingual similarity and candidate detection but is repeatedly humbled by strong classical baselines (Eklund et al. 2023; Meaney/Alex/Lamb 2024).

## Details

### (a) Indexing / ontologies / classification schemes
- **ATU & TMI (foundational).** ATU = Hans-Jörg Uther, *The Types of International Folktales* (2004, FFC 284–286); TMI = Stith Thompson, *Motif-Index of Folk-Literature* (1955–58). These are the reference vocabularies mythoscope should adopt as a controlled index.
- **MOMFER** (Karsdorp, van der Meulen, Meder & van den Bosch, *Folklore* 126:1, 2015, pp. 37–52; DOI 10.1080/0015587X.2015.1006954). A search engine over the TMI that adds **WordNet-based semantic query expansion**, allowing users to "search the Motif-Index for semantic concepts, such as 'mythical animals', 'mortality', or 'emotions'." A single query like `wn:monster` retrieves all monster motifs spread across TMI subheadings — dragons, serpents, griffins, werewolves, chimeras, unicorns — and extracts each motif's attested geographical locations. This is the single most direct precedent for what mythoscope wants: semantic (not string) search over a motif index. Live at folgertkarsdorp.nl/momfer.
- **Declerck's ontologies (DFKI/Saarland).** A sustained line of work converting TMI and ATU into interoperable **OWL/RDF(S)/SKOS** with multilingual (English, German, Russian, Bulgarian) labels via the lemon model, plus a Proppian-function ontology (Declerck & Lendvai 2011; Koleva, Declerck & Krieger 2012; Declerck, Kostová & Schäfer 2017 "Towards a Linked Data Access…", DH2017; Declerck et al. 2017, LT4DHCSEE/RANLP). The ATU ontology comprised 2,802 classes organized into seven main subclasses matching Uther's hierarchy. **Caveat:** despite the extensive publication record, no publicly downloadable RDF/OWL file or SPARQL endpoint for these ontologies could be verified — a developer would likely need to contact DFKI directly.
- **Ontology of Greek Mythology (OGM)** (Pastor-Sánchez, Kontopoulos, Saorín, Bebis & Darányi, *Semantic Web Journal*, 2021). Built from 5,377 Wikidata items using 34 selected properties (of 283); includes explicit representation of *contradictions* between statements and a SPARQL retrieval tool. **Digital LIMC** (openhumanitiesdata, johd.403) is a complementary RDF knowledge graph: ~3,000 figures on ~56,000 objects, 40,500 images, 21.6M RDF triples. FactGrid/Roscher's Lexikon LOD project (CC-0) is an emerging mythology LOD hub.
- **Strengths/limits for mythoscope:** indices give a stable, expert-curated label space and cross-cultural cross-references; but they are Eurocentric (widely criticized: Dundes 1997; Jason 2006), coarse, and "formally fluid" — motifs overlap and resist crisp boundaries.

### (b) Supervised classification of tale types / motifs
- Meder, Karsdorp, Nguyen, Theune, Trieschnigg & Muiser (2016), "Automatic Enrichment and Classification of Folktales in the Dutch Folktale Database" (*JAF* 129) — learning-to-rank for story-type classification.
- **Eklund, Hagedorn & Darányi (2023)**, "Teaching Tale Types to a Computer," *Fabula* 64:1–2, pp. 92–106 — SVM with TF-IDF over the 10 best-populated classes of the Annotated Folktales collection (1,518 texts, 182 ATU types), F-scores ~0.8–1.0 (no baseline reported).
- **Meaney, Alex & Lamb (2024)**, NLP4DH (aclanthology 2024.nlp4dh-1.31) — ATU classification + narrator-gender prediction for Irish/Scottish Gaelic; mBERT/XLM-R/gaBERT vs. an SVM baseline that remained competitive. A "BERT-Based Classification Model: The Case of Russian Fairy Tales" (*JLE*) is a comparable effort.
- **Takeaway:** on small folktale corpora, non-contextual baselines (SVM+TF-IDF) repeatedly rival or beat neural/LLM approaches — a critical warning for mythoscope's evaluation design.

### (c) Topic modeling (LDA → BERTopic)
- Karsdorp & van den Bosch (2013), "Identifying Motifs in Folktales Using Topic Models" (BENELEARN) — the origin point for treating motifs as latent topics.
- **Tangherlini & Chen (2024)**, "Travels with BERT," *Orbis Litterarum* 79:519–562 (DOI 10.1111/oli.12458) — **BERTopic** with a fine-tuned 19th-c. Danish LLM embedding model, HDBSCAN clustering, UMAP reduction, class-based TF-IDF + KeyBERT labeling, to map intertextuality across Andersen's fairy tales and travel writing. This is a strong template for mythoscope's clustering/labeling pipeline (they set `min_cluster_size` of 7 for small subcorpora, 10 for large; use `reduce_outliers` to relabel HDBSCAN's "-1" noise cluster).

### (d) Embedding / similarity retrieval & motif induction
- **Arčon, Robnik-Šikonja & Tratnik (2025)**, "Large language models for folktale type automation based on motifs: Cinderella case study" (arXiv 2510.18561; *Fabula*). GPT-4.5 zero-shot yes/no motif presence over ATU 510A on 13→77→33 Cinderella variants (English + Slovene), analyzing three motif sets (15 basic, 18 extended, 14 "supermotifs"), plus **LaBSE embeddings + HDBSCAN** for semantic similarity. Demonstrates the exact embedding-clustering pattern mythoscope targets, cross-lingually. Paper is CC BY 4.0; no public code repository could be located.
- **Karsdorp & Fonteyn (2019)**, "Cultural entrenchment of folktales is encoded in language" (*Palgrave Communications*); Karsdorp et al. (2015) "Animacy Detection in Stories" (CMN, OASIcs 45) — showed word2vec/skip-gram embeddings alone achieve near-optimal animacy detection and produce interpretable t-SNE semantic maps of characters (incl. a "Supernatural" cluster). Directly relevant to character/entity extraction.
- **Yarlott et al. (2021), "Finding Trolls Under Bridges"** (arXiv 2204.06085; 9th Advances in Cognitive Systems, ACS 2021) — a dedicated motif *detector*; an off-the-shelf metaphor detector as a feature "achieves an F1 of 0.35 on motifs and a macro-average F1 of 0.21 across four categories" assigned to motif candidates (best sub-result F1=0.59 for referential usage), illustrating how hard automated motif detection remains.
- Hatzel & Biemann (2024) "Story Embeddings" — argues narrative similarity requires modeling beyond keyword/sentence overlap toward narrative structure; relevant to mythoscope's retrieval quality ceiling.

### (e) Phylogenetic / evolutionary methods
- **da Silva & Tehrani (2016)**, "Comparative phylogenetic analyses uncover the ancient roots of Indo-European folktales," *Royal Society Open Science* 3:150645 — coded (in a released Excel matrix, Table S1) the "presence (1)/absence (0) of 275 Magic Tale types found among 50 Indo-European-speaking populations." After two horizontal-transmission tests, 199 tales were discarded; 100 patterned by linguistic relatedness; and only ATU 330 "The Smith and the Devil" reconstructed robustly to Proto-Indo-European. Bayesian phylogenetics + autologistic controls for geography.
- **d'Huy** — phylogenetic reconstructions of the Cosmic Hunt (Cahiers de l'AARS 16, 2013) and Polyphemus (ATU 1137, 2013); used BEAST, MrBayes, NeighborNet.
- **Thuillard, d'Huy, Le Quellec & Berezkin (2018)**, "A Large-Scale Study of World Myths," *Trames* 22(4):407–424 — phylogenetic networks over Berezkin's ~2,264 motifs / 934 peoples.
- **Sakamoto Martini, Kendal & Tehrani (2023)** — phylomemetic Cinderella study (ATU 510/511), 266 versions, Bayesian inference + NeighborNet.
- **Kestemont, Karsdorp et al. (2022)**, "Forgotten books," *Science* 375(6582):765–769 — unseen-species (ecology) models (Chao estimators) to estimate lost medieval narrative diversity; a striking cross-disciplinary import (project site forgotten-books.netlify.app).
- **Limit:** these methods require pre-coded presence/absence matrices — the coding itself is the bottleneck mythoscope could help automate.

### (f) Network analysis
- **Mac Carron & Kenna (2012)**, "Universal Properties of Mythological Networks," *EPL* 99:28002 (Coventry) — character networks of the Iliad, Beowulf, Táin Bó Cúailnge vs. fictional controls (Les Misérables, Richard III, Fellowship of the Ring, Harry Potter). Giant components covered ~98% of nodes for the Táin and Iliad vs. 68% for Beowulf; the Iliad was "most similar to a real social network," Beowulf a reality/fiction mix (small-world but disassortative, becoming assortative when the protagonist is removed), and the Táin "most fictitious," with its artificiality traceable to just six highly-connected characters hypothesized to be amalgams. Follow-ons: the Odyssey mythological network (*PLoS ONE* 2018, e0200703), Kyiv bylyny cycle, Icelandic sagas.
- **Abello, Broadwell & Tangherlini (2012)**, "Computational Folkloristics" (*Communications of the ACM*) and "Disentangling the Folklore Hairball" (2023) — motif/tale-type and story networks over the 30,000+ story Evald Tang Kristensen Danish corpus.

### (g) Narrative structure / Propp function extraction
- **Finlayson**: Analogical Story Merging (2009/2012 MIT thesis; *JAF* 2016 "Inferring Propp's Functions from Semantically Annotated Text," 129:511, 55–77) — Bayesian model-merging over 15 deeply-annotated Propp tales; recovered A/a (villainy/lack), H/I (struggle/victory), W (reward) with high accuracy. **ProppLearner** corpus (DSH 2017, 32:2, 284–300). Tooling via the Story Workbench.
- **ProppOntology / Pannach (2019)** — ontology-driven Proppian system tested on southern African tales (Göttingen MSc).
- **Tagging Narrative with Propp's Character Functions Using LLMs (2024, CEUR Vol-3671, paper 12)** — explicitly reports **negative results**, confirming LLMs do not yet reliably label Propp functions zero/few-shot.

### (h) LLM-based annotation / extraction frontiers
- LLM zero-shot motif presence detection (Arčon et al. 2025) works for well-defined motif checklists on a known type but has not been validated for open-vocabulary motif discovery.
- T5-based folktale generation; RNN/LSTM West African folktale generation and classification (Lô, de Boer & van Aart 2020, corpus of 742 West African + Western European tales, where a bag-of-words MLP beat an LSTM — again a small-data warning).
- **Cross-cutting caution:** narrative "flattening" and post-training compression of thematic/affective/stylistic variation is a documented risk when using aligned LLM embeddings for fiction.

## Recommendations (for mythoscope)
1. **Adopt ATU + TMI as the controlled index/label space, and Berezkin's catalogue for world-mythology coverage.** Ingest the aft "trilogy" dataframes (CC-BY-SA) as your seed TMI/ATU tables and ATU-annotated tale texts. This gives you an expert cross-reference layer to anchor embedding search — exactly the MOMFER model, upgraded from WordNet expansion to dense retrieval.
2. **Build the retrieval layer on sentence embeddings + approximate NN, with HDBSCAN/UMAP for motif induction.** Use a multilingual model (LaBSE, or a strong multilingual E5/BGE) so cross-lingual motif alignment works out of the box — validated by Arčon et al. (2025) and Tangherlini & Chen (2024). Store passage-level embeddings; motifs live at sub-document granularity. Reuse the BERTopic pattern (embeddings → UMAP → HDBSCAN → class-based TF-IDF + KeyBERT labels, with outlier reduction) for interpretable motif clusters.
3. **Treat LLMs as candidate-generators and annotators, not classifiers.** Use LLM zero/few-shot for (a) motif-presence checks against a *known* checklist and (b) draft TMI/ATU labels for human confirmation. Do NOT rely on them for open-vocabulary motif discovery or Propp function tagging (documented negative results).
4. **Always ship a TF-IDF/SVM baseline.** The consistent finding (Eklund 2023; Meaney 2024; Lô 2020) is that simple baselines match neural methods on small folktale corpora. If your embeddings don't beat TF-IDF on held-out ATU classification, that's a signal, not noise.
5. **Add a network view.** Character-network extraction (via animacy + coreference, à la Karsdorp) and motif co-occurrence networks (à la Abello/Tangherlini) are cheap, interpretable value-adds over pure similarity search.
6. **Respect licensing.** FairytaleQA (Apache-2.0) and aft (CC-BY-SA) are safe to build on; folkloredatabase.com explicitly forbids scraping/AI use; MFTD has no license/API (scrape cautiously and attribute); Berezkin is best accessed via the mythology-queries engine.

**Benchmarks that would change the plan:** if a fine-tuned multilingual embedding model exceeds ~0.85 macro-F1 on aft's 10 best-populated ATU classes AND beats the SVM baseline, promote embeddings from candidate-generation to primary classification. If LLM zero-shot motif-presence agreement with expert annotation exceeds ~0.8 F1 on a held-out *multi-type* set (versus Arčon's single-type ATU-510A demo), expand LLM use to open motif detection.

## Caveats
- **Motif detection is unsolved.** Motifs are "formally fluid"; there is no large gold-standard motif-annotated corpus; reported successes are on narrow, single-type checklists, and the best dedicated detector (Yarlott et al.) reached only macro-F1 ≈ 0.21.
- **Small-data regime.** Folktale corpora are small and stylistically homogeneous; overfitting and baseline-beating are recurring problems.
- **Index bias.** ATU/TMI are Eurocentric and coarse (Dundes: tale types "overlap"; more than half of types are effectively a single motif); Berezkin uses an incompatible motif definition. Cross-scheme alignment is non-trivial.
- **Some tools are unavailable/undocumented.** Declerck's TMI/ATU ontologies, the Cinderella LLM paper's code, the Gaelic folktale data, and ProppLearner have no verified public downloads. WitchHunter and etKspace are research tools, not distributed software.
- **Reproducibility gaps.** Several high-profile results (Eklund's F-scores without baselines; phylogenetic reconstructions sensitive to coding choices, e.g. only ATU 330 surviving da Silva & Tehrani's controls) warrant caution.
- **Report scope.** Where counts vary across sources (Berezkin motif totals; corpus sizes), figures are version-dependent snapshots; treat them as approximate and re-verify against the live source before ingestion.
