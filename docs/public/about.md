# About Mythoscope

**Mythoscope — Collaborative Semantic Archaeology.**

Mythoscope is an interdisciplinary research initiative and open analytical platform for the
large-scale comparative study of mythology, ancient religions, and cultural texts. It
integrates classical humanities methods with computational approaches so that scholars can
explore deep semantic structures, trace cultural patterns across traditions, and
investigate the historical evolution of symbolic systems — reproducibly, and at a scale no
single reader can reach.

It sits at three intersections at once: **antiquity** (myth, archaeology, heritage),
**modern science** (AI, digital humanities, open infrastructure), and **collectivity**
(international collaboration, openness). Think of it as three layers of one thing: a
*conceptual* layer — semantic archaeology, the guiding philosophy; a *methodological*
layer — the computational framework; and an *infrastructural* layer — Mythoscope itself,
the collaborative platform.

## Why "Mythoscope"?

The suffix *-scope* is not just a word for an instrument. In the scientific culture of the
nineteenth century it marked a specific epistemic turn — the moment knowledge began to be
produced through instruments of observation. The *microscope*, the *telescope*, the
*spectroscope*, the *oscilloscope*: each of these names belongs to the moment a science
began to reveal a level of reality that had been invisible — the microworld, the cosmos,
the spectrum, the electrical signal.

The telescope reshaped astronomy; the microscope reshaped biology. *Mythoscope* proposes
something analogous for the study of culture: an instrument that makes visible what was
previously hidden inside texts and traditions. It moves the study of myth from a purely
descriptive discipline toward an instrumental one — as if mythology had been given its own
lens. And, in the spirit of science and technology studies, an instrument does not merely
show reality; it *shapes how we see it*. The lens is part of the argument. That is why the
name is paired with **Semantic Archaeology** — to keep the tie to the interpretive
tradition, and to prevent the project from reading as a purely technocratic exercise. This
is a new optics for an old question, not a replacement for reading.

## Why collaborative infrastructure for discovery

Behind the tool is a manifesto, and it rests on four claims.

**1. Scale reveals what examples hide.** Lévi-Strauss analysed dozens of myths; Campbell
analysed hundreds; Mythoscope is built to work across tens of thousands. A different scale
makes different patterns visible.

**2. Algorithms see what humans can't.** People notice explicit similarities; algorithms
detect latent semantic patterns and reveal hidden groupings. Computational methods do not
replace human interpretation — they complement it, surfacing structure for a scholar to
judge.

**3. Collaboration transcends individual bias.** Every scholar carries theoretical
commitments, and a single perspective misses cultural specifics. Distributed expertise
provides triangulation; the knowledge of a community exceeds the knowledge of any one
person.

**4. Open infrastructure outlives individual projects.** Theories come and go;
infrastructure persists. Tools built well enough to let *future* researchers ask new
questions are tools for discovery, not merely for verifying what we already believe.

### The deductive-to-inductive shift

Traditional comparative mythology runs deductively: a scholar takes a limited corpus and
tests a hypothesis against a handful of examples. Mythoscope inverts the order. It starts
from a broad corpus, applies computational analysis so that patterns can *emerge*, and then
turns to community interpretation to contextualise and validate what surfaced — theory
generation rather than theory confirmation. The infrastructure is designed for discovery,
not verification.

That ambition carries a discipline with it, and it is the anchor of the whole project. A
field that reports only its successes cannot be trusted with a question as seductive as the
origin of myth, because there will always be some transformation of the data under which a
compelling pattern appears. So Mythoscope states its assumptions in the open where they can
be attacked, corrects for the uneven history of collecting at every step, sets tests that a
candidate method must pass *before* it is adopted, and reports the clean negatives — the
things that did not work — as plainly as the findings. Discovery, held to that standard, is
worth more than confirmation.

## Contribute

Mythoscope is being built as open infrastructure by a global community of scholars,
technologists, and culture specialists — and it is early. Contributions of corpus, expert
annotation, non-textual datasets (geographic, temporal, archaeological), methods, code, and
critical interpretation are all welcome, under principles of open science, fair
attribution, and respect for cultural protocols and Indigenous sovereignty. No single
scholar, institution, or nation can answer fundamental questions about human mythology
alone. That is why this is collaborative.
