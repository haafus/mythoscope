The 14 regions — a corpus-first map of the world's mythologies

Mythoscope organises every tradition it holds under one top-level classification: region, a set of fourteen values — a two-level model, region → tradition, where a region groups many traditions and a tradition is a single mythology such as Greek, Norse, or Yoruba.

Why the regions are drawn from the corpus, not the index

The taxonomy is built on an assembled full-text corpus, not on a motif index. That single commitment shapes everything downstream:

  • “From the data” means differences between the texts themselves — content, language, and genre — not counts of motif attestations.
  • The areal scaffolding is provisional: Berezkin's groupings are a useful starting scaffold, not the authority.
  • Obtainability constrains what fills each region first: the literate Old World is build-ready; the oral world waits on OCR and curation.

Four criteria that routinely conflict

  1. Geography — does the region map to a compact, unambiguous area?
  2. Cultural commonality — are its traditions genuinely kin, not merely neighbours?
  3. Expressiveness and clarity — recognizable names, a sensible count, no artificial merges.
  4. Volume — balance measured against the corpus, by traditions and actual full-text volume.

Where distinctiveness and documentation conflict, documentation wins the granularity call.

The fourteen regions

#RegionIn briefExample traditions
1Sub-Saharan AfricaThe world's densest concentration of distinct oral traditions, across unrelated language phyla.Yoruba, Igbo, Akan, Dogon, Zulu, San
2Near East & North AfricaThe earliest literate mythologies and the Abrahamic homelands.Sumerian, Egyptian, Hittite, Jewish, Islamic
3EuropePredominantly Indo-European with Uralic Finno-Ugric; deep PIE roots.Greek, Roman, Norse, Celtic, Slavic, Finnish
4Caucasus & IranThe Iranian/Persianate world plus the Caucasus shatter zone.Persian, Scythian, Ossetian (Nart), Armenian
5Inner AsiaThe Turco-Mongol nomadic steppe — shamanic, Tengrist, great oral epics.Kyrgyz (Manas), Mongol (Geser), Yakut, Tibetan
6South AsiaThe single largest textual tradition on Earth; source of the Indosphere.Vedic, Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, Dravidian
7Mainland Southeast AsiaThe Indianized mainland; Hindu-Buddhist classical myth.Burmese, Thai, Khmer, Vietnamese, Hmong
8East AsiaThe Sinosphere — the clean CJK core.Chinese, Korean, Japanese (Shinto), Ainu
9AustronesiaOne language family, one Neolithic maritime expansion.Javanese, Batak, Filipino, Polynesian, Malagasy
10Papua & AustraliaThe non-Austronesian deep-Pacific Sahul population.Aboriginal Australian, Papuan highland groups
11Circumpolar NorthThe continuous Arctic/boreal belt across the Bering Strait.Chukchi, Evenki, Inuit, Aleut, Northern Cree
12Native North AmericaIndigenous North America outside the Arctic.Iroquois, Lakota, Navajo, Hopi, Haida
13Mesoamerica & AndesThe literate, state-level New-World myth complexes.Aztec, Maya, Mixtec, Inca/Quechua, Aymara
14Lowland South AmericaAmazonia and the Southern Cone; the terminal end of the human expansion.Tupí/Guaraní, Carib, Yanomami, Mapuche

The colour system

Each region carries one base colour; per-tradition shades are derived from it in OKLCH, so the palette is colour-blind-robust by construction rather than by hand-tuning.
