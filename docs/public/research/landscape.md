# The landscape of computational folkloristics

Who does this work, and where does it appear? This page maps the research community behind
the computational study of folk narrative and myth: the labs and people to track, the
journals and conferences the work lands in, and a starter reading list of landmark
publications. It is the companion to the [field survey](computational-folkloristics.md),
which covers the methods and their history; two further pages cover [where the corpora come
from](corpus-sourcing.md) and [how the great encyclopedias carve the world](encyclopedias.md).
For how these threads are assembled into a working tool, see [How it works](../how-it-works.md).

Two overlapping fields run through everything below:

- **Computational folkloristics** — the quantitative and NLP study of folk narrative (tale
  types, motifs, archives, variation, geography). The term was popularised by Tangherlini.
- **Computational or statistical (phylogenetic) mythology** — treating myths as evolving
  lineages of "mythemes," reconstructing ancestry and diffusion with tools borrowed from
  evolutionary biology.

## People and labs to track

- **Timothy R. Tangherlini** (UC Berkeley; previously UCLA) — coined and championed
  *computational folkloristics*. GIS, network, and topic-model analysis of Danish folklore
  archives (the "Danish Folklore Nexus" and WitchHunter work) and the notion of the
  *Folklore Macroscope*. The field's central figure.
- **The Meertens Institute (KNAW), Amsterdam** — the folk-narrative computing hub, with
  **Folgert Karsdorp** (head of Ethnology & Oral Culture), **Theo Meder**, **Marten van der
  Meulen**, and **Dong Nguyen**. Home of MOMFER, the Dutch Folktale Database (100,000+
  ATU-typed tales), and work on automatic genre classification, enrichment, and animacy
  detection.
- **Mark A. Finlayson** (Florida International University, Cognac Lab) — automatic motif
  detection and indexing, ProppLearner, the Story Workbench, and the (pending) Arabian
  Nights / El-Shamy motif corpus.
- **The phylogenetic-mythology group** — **Julien d'Huy**, **Jean-Loïc Le Quellec**, **Marc
  Thuillard**, and **Yuri Berezkin**: myths as evolving mythemes, the Cosmic Hunt
  reconstruction, large-scale world-myth mapping, and Berezkin's areal motif catalogue.
- **Jamshid J. Tehrani** (Durham) and **Sara Graça da Silva** — phylogenetic analysis of
  folktales over the ATU catalogue (Little Red Riding Hood; the "Smith and the Devil"
  reconstruction).
- **Karl Grossner** (GLOS — Geographic Lens on Stories) — embeddings and LLMs over digitised
  TMI/ATU plus geography.
- **Thierry Declerck** (DFKI, Saarbrücken) — TMI and ATU as Linked Open Data (OWL/RDF).
- **Gerhard Lauer** (Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz, Institute of Book Science) —
  computational folktale studies at scale (large corpora, fairy tales, machine learning);
  author of the 2023 *Fabula* survey that is the field's best short entry point.
- **Emese Ilyefalvi** (Digital Folklore Lab, Hungary) — digital folklore databases,
  metadata and standardisation, corpus analysis of archives.
- **Pádraig Mac Carron and Ralph Kenna** (Coventry) — *network mythology*: quantitative
  comparison of epic character networks (Iliad, Beowulf, Táin).
- **Berkeley Institute for Data Science (BIDS)** with Slovenian folklore researchers —
  recent AI/LLM-for-folklore collaboration under the AI4DH banner.

## Journals

**Field-specific (folk narrative):**

- **Fabula** (De Gruyter, since 1958; organ of the ISFNR) — the journal of comparative
  folk-narrative studies (German/English/French).
- **Journal of American Folklore** (American Folklore Society, since 1888) — host of the
  2016 special issue *Big Folklore: A Special Issue on Computational Folkloristics*
  (guest-edited by Tangherlini).
- **Journal of Folklore Research** (Indiana University Press) — explicitly welcomes
  digital-humanities work.
- **Folklore** (Taylor & Francis) — published the MOMFER paper (2015).
- **Journal of Ethnology and Folkloristics**; **Western Folklore**.

**Computational and methods venues (where the CS-heavy work lands):**

- **Computational Humanities Research (CHR)** — an open-access journal (Cambridge Core) plus
  annual conference; the primary home for quantitative humanities.
- **Journal of Cultural Analytics** — the computational study of culture.
- **Journal of Open Humanities Data (JOHD)** — published the trilogy dataset paper *Bearing
  a Bag-of-Tales* (2022).
- **Digital Scholarship in the Humanities (DSH)** (Oxford/ADHO) and **Digital Humanities
  Quarterly (DHQ)** — general digital humanities.
- **Communications of the ACM** — carried Abello, Broadwell & Tangherlini's *Computational
  Folkloristics* (2012).

**Comparative-mythology and statistical:**

- **Nouvelle Mythologie Comparée / New Comparative Mythology** — d'Huy et al., *Computational
  Approaches to Myths Analysis: Cosmic Hunt* (2018).
- **Trames** — *A Large-Scale Study of World Myths* (2018; Thuillard, d'Huy, Berezkin, Le
  Quellec).
- **RMN Newsletter** (Retrospective Methods Network, Helsinki) — statistical methods for
  mythology.
- Cross-over venues for the phylogenetic and network papers: **Royal Society Open Science**,
  **PLOS ONE**, **Current Biology**, **EPJ Data Science**, and **EPL (Europhysics Letters)**.

## Conferences and workshops

- **Computational Humanities Research (CHR)** — the key annual venue; proceedings on
  CEUR-WS. The 2027 edition is at the University of Manchester, 5–8 January 2027
  (submissions due 2026-08-14).
- **Digital Humanities (DH / ADHO)** — the flagship annual DH conference (where DFKI's DH2017
  Linked Open Data paper appeared).
- **ISFNR Congress** — the International Society for Folk Narrative Research; the discipline's
  global meeting.
- **LaTeCH-CLfL** — the Workshop on Language Technology for Cultural Heritage and for
  Computational Linguistics for Literature (ACL/EACL/COLING-affiliated); the main NLP-side
  workshop for narrative and heritage text.
- **AAAI Workshop on Computational Folkloristics** (2016) — the field's named CS workshop
  (Tangherlini, Broadwell).
- **AI Methods for Research of Folkloristic Narratives** — a recent international workshop
  (University of Ljubljana, 2025; BIDS and Slovenia).
- **ACM/IEEE Joint Conference on Digital Libraries (JCDL)** and the **Association for
  Computers and the Humanities (ACH) Conference** — regular homes for archive/tooling and
  DH-methods work.
- **CLARIN / DARIAH** annual events — European DH research-infrastructure venues where
  folktale-database and tooling work is presented.

A note on the shape of the field: there is **no dedicated computational-folkloristics
conference**. The work is spread across the venues above, with DH/ADHO and CHR the primary
two.

## A landmark reading list

**Computational folkloristics — foundational:**

- Abello, Broadwell & Tangherlini, *Computational Folkloristics*, *Communications of the ACM*
  55(7), 2012 — the discipline-defining programmatic paper (folklore-as-data, semantic
  networks, knowledge graphs, large-scale motif analysis, GIS).
- Tangherlini, *The Folklore Macroscope* (2013) — the conceptual shift from close reading of
  single texts to "macroscope" analysis of millions at once.
- *Computing Folklore Studies: Mapping over a Century of Scholarly Production through
  Topics*, *Journal of American Folklore* 126(502):455–475, 2013 — topic-modelling the
  field's own history.
- Tangherlini (guest ed.), *Big Folklore: A Special Issue on Computational Folkloristics*,
  *Journal of American Folklore* 129(511), 2016 — the single best issue to read.
- **Gerhard Lauer, *Computational Folktale Studies: A Very Brief History*, *Fabula*
  64(1–2):1–6, 2023** — the best modern overview and a recommended first read (Aarne →
  Thompson → ATU → DH → NLP → LLM).
- Emese Ilyefalvi, *The theoretical, methodological and technical issues of digital folklore
  databases and computational folkloristics* (2018) — on digital archives, motif databases,
  and data standardisation.
- Karsdorp, van der Meulen, Meder & van den Bosch, *MOMFER: A Search Engine of Thompson's
  Motif-Index*, *Folklore* 126(1), 2015.
- Nguyen, Trieschnigg, Meder & Theune — automatic classification and enrichment of
  folk-narrative genres in the Dutch Folktale Database.
- Hagedorn & Darányi, *Bearing a Bag-of-Tales*, *Journal of Open Humanities Data*, 2022 — the
  trilogy corpus.
- Declerck et al., *Towards a Linked Data Access to Folktales classified by Thompson's Motifs
  and ATU Types*, DH2017.

**Phylogenetic folktale and myth analysis:**

- Tehrani, *The Phylogeny of Little Red Riding Hood*, *PLOS ONE* 8(11), 2013.
- da Silva & Tehrani, *Comparative phylogenetic analyses uncover the ancient roots of
  Indo-European folktales*, *Royal Society Open Science* 3(1), 2016 (ATU 330, "Smith and the
  Devil").
- Thuillard, Le Quellec, d'Huy, *Computational Approaches to Myths Analysis: Cosmic Hunt*,
  *Nouvelle Mythologie Comparée* 4, 2018.
- Thuillard, d'Huy, Berezkin & Le Quellec, *A Large-Scale Study of World Myths*, *Trames*
  22(4), 2018 (2,264 motifs / 40,000 myths / 934 cultures).
- Finlayson et al. — motif detection and the Arabian Nights corpus (2026).

**Network mythology:**

- Mac Carron & Kenna, *Universal Properties of Mythological Networks*, *EPL (Europhysics
  Letters)* 99(2), 2012 — character-interaction networks of the Iliad, Beowulf, and the Táin
  compared to real social networks.
- Kenna, Mac Carron et al., *Maths Meets Myths: Quantitative Approaches to Ancient
  Narratives* (2016) — the edited volume of this strand.

## Methods in play

The community's active toolkit, in brief:

- **Type/motif classification and auto-tagging** — supervised and LLM classification of
  tales into ATU types and motifs (Meertens, Finlayson).
- **Phylogenetic and network reconstruction** — myths and tales as character matrices; trees
  plus phylogenetic networks (d'Huy, Tehrani, Thuillard).
- **Embeddings and semantic search** — vector representations of motif and type text (MOMFER,
  GLOS).
- **GIS and spatial diffusion** — mapping tale and motif distributions and areal clustering
  (Tangherlini, Berezkin, GLOS).
- **Linked Open Data and ontologies** — TMI/ATU as RDF/OWL, with cross-index links (DFKI).
- **Network analysis** — storyteller–story networks and motif co-occurrence graphs.
- **Network mythology** — character-interaction graphs of epics and myths, compared to real
  versus fictional social networks (Mac Carron & Kenna).

The frontier of computational mythology is where much of the new energy sits: phylogenetic
reconstruction of myths; knowledge graphs of gods, characters, and motifs; automatic motif
extraction; semantic embeddings of mythological characters; narrative graphs; LLM-based
comparison of mythological corpora; large motif graphs (GraphRAG-style); and multimodal
myth-story databases. The common thread is a move **from static motif catalogues to dynamic
semantic networks** linking motifs, plots, characters, and the geography of their spread.

The corpora these groups repeatedly test on — detailed in [where the corpora come
from](corpus-sourcing.md) — include the Nederlandse Volksverhalenbank and ISEBEL, Iceland's
Sagnagrunnur, Germany's WossiDiA, Estonia's ETKSpace, and Tangherlini's Danish Folklore
Nexus.
