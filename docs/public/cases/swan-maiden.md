# The Swan-Maiden: A Motif Too Broad to Date

A man comes upon women bathing, their feather or seal-skin garments laid aside on the shore. He hides one. The woman who cannot reclaim her skin can neither fly nor swim away, and so she becomes his wife — until, years later, she finds the hidden garment and is gone.

This is the swan-maiden, the type case of Berezkin's **magic wife (K25)**, defined broadly as *a man consciously marrying a woman related to the non-human world*. With **513 attesting traditions**, it is one of the most widely recorded story-shapes in the entire catalogue, found from Ireland to Japan to the Americas. It is exactly the kind of near-universal motif that has tempted scholars to see a single deep origin behind it.

## What the machine sees

Run through Mythoscope, the swan-maiden's distribution is broad and its footprint disjunct. It touches multiple continental sets, and on the depth score of our antiquity analysis it reads as old. Plotted on the map, its 513 traditions do not form one blanket but several separated regional clusters — a European mass, an East and Southeast Asian mass, and scattered American and Oceanic outliers — each drawn as its own smoothed contour, with lone strays left bare so that a single outlier cannot balloon a shape. That visible disjunction — gaps between filled regions rather than a continuous spread — is precisely the footprint the depth score is built to reward.

But that is only one reading. A second method reads the language tree, and it finds the swan-maiden's carriers *scattered* rather than clustered: it does not track descent within any one family. Put the two readings through the gated combination and the motif files as **areal-broad**; the continuous mixture model puts its descent component at about **0.16**. On the numbers, this is a widely diffused motif — spread sideways across the world by contact and re-telling, not inherited down a single lineage.

## The honest limit

Here an abstract caveat becomes concrete. That descent figure of 0.16 is *the same* one the machine assigns to a motif of utterly different character — the pan-global pairing of [sun and moon as kin](sun-and-moon.md). Everything about the swan-maiden says *travelled*; everything about that celestial pairing says *ancient*; and to every instrument the distribution affords, the two are indistinguishable.

The swan-maiden also exposes a limit of our infrastructure: its tale-type, **ATU 400**, was one of the correspondences [the crosswalk](../crosswalk.md) missed, so its cross-catalogue corroboration is a lower bound rather than a ceiling.

The verdict is deliberately modest. The machine can say the swan-maiden is broad and areal. It cannot, from its distribution alone, say how old it is. That is not a softness in the method but the edge of what a map can know.
