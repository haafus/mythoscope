# Updates & Notes

A running notebook for the project: short dispatches on what the machine is turning up,
what we are building, and what we are still stuck on. It is less formal than the
[working paper](publications.md) and more concrete than a roadmap — a place to show a single
motif, a single prototype, or a single result in the time it takes to read a coffee.

*This is a living page.* New notes are added here and, for readers who want them delivered,
go out through the newsletter. Two recurring series anchor it:

- **Motif of the month** — one motif followed through the whole apparatus: its
  distribution, what the depth score makes of it, and whether the machine can date it or
  has to admit it cannot. The point is to make the aggregate results of the paper
  legible one case at a time.
- **Mockup of the month** — one standalone prototype built over the motif data. These are
  self-contained feature experiments — a way to try an interaction or a view on the
  crosswalk before it is worth folding into the main app. Each note links the prototype and
  says plainly what it does and does not yet do.

Notes are dated, newest first. Numbers and claims are drawn from the project's findings; see
[Publications](publications.md) for the fuller argument and [How it works](how-it-works.md)
for the method behind the figures.

## Motif of the month — August 2026 · The fished-up earth

Most widely-shared motifs defeat any attempt to date them: a deep inheritance and a wide
diffusion cast the same broad, scattered shadow, and no score computed from a distribution
can tell them apart. So it is worth starting the series with a **counter-case** — a motif
the machine genuinely cracks.

Berezkin's **fished-up earth (B4)** — *islands or a continent are fished out of the ocean,
or pulled to their present place by a rope* — is a cousin of the earth-diver. With just
**36 attesting traditions** it is far narrower than the near-universal motifs, and, crucially,
its carriers are **not** scattered across the language tree. They cluster, within the
Austronesian world, in a way the method reads as *descent* rather than diffusion — and that
single fact unlocks everything the rest of the machinery can otherwise only wish for.

Because B4 behaves like an inheritance, it can be dated by clade depth. Reconstructing its
origin places its centroid in **Western Oceania**, with a ceiling at the Austronesian
expansion of about **5,200 years ago**, and mapping its spread draws lines fanning out
across the Pacific along the paths of that expansion. (One small cartographic care makes the
picture legible: the map is re-centred on the motif's own region, so a Pacific-spread
distribution reads as a coherent fan *around* the ocean rather than being torn in two by the
Atlantic seam of a standard projection.)

The estimate is honest about its resolution — a family-level point estimate, not a
node-consistent reconstruction with full uncertainty; a properly dated tree with Bayesian
phylogeography would sharpen it. But even at family resolution, B4 is the fossil-record
dream of comparative mythology realised for once: not just *that* a motif was inherited but
roughly *when*, *from where*, and along which routes. It stands for the roughly one percent
of the corpus that behaves like the fossil the whole enterprise was built to read.

*Full case study:* [The fished-up earth](cases/fished-up-earth.md).

## Note — August 2026 · When two motifs cast the same shadow

A companion to the above, and the more sobering half of the picture. Set two motifs side by
side:

- The **swan-maiden** (Berezkin's *magic wife*, K25) — a man marries a woman of the
  non-human world, who leaves once she recovers her hidden feather or seal-skin. With
  **513 attesting traditions**, from Ireland to Japan to the Americas, it is one of the most
  widely recorded story-shapes in the catalogue. The machine reads it as **broad and
  areal**: spread sideways by contact and re-telling.
- **Sun and moon as kin** (Berezkin's A3) — the sun imagined as male, the moon as female —
  attested in **362 traditions**, pan-global and cross-hemispheric. It is cosmological to
  its core and a leading candidate for the deep celestial substrate, surviving every control
  that strips inflated antiquity off lesser motifs.

Everything about A3 says *ancient*; everything about the swan-maiden says *travelled*. And
yet the mixture model gives them **the same decomposition** — a descent component of about
0.16 apiece. To every instrument a distribution affords, the pan-global cosmological pairing
that may be tens of millennia old and the fairy-tale that may be a few thousand years young
are **indistinguishable**.

This is not a softness in one method; it is a wall the evidence itself puts up. A deep shared
inheritance and a wide diffusion cast the same shadow — broad, disjunct, areally scattered —
and no score computed from that shadow can separate them. Past a certain point, *where a
motif is found* has told us everything it can, and the rest must come from outside:
genetics, dated corridors, dated trees. Reporting that boundary plainly, rather than fitting
a compelling story to noise, is the discipline the whole programme rests on.

*Full case studies:* [The swan-maiden](cases/swan-maiden.md) · [Sun and moon as
kin](cases/sun-and-moon.md).
