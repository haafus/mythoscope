The Fished-Up Earth: The Motif the Machine Cracks

Islands, or a whole continent, are fished out of the ocean — or pulled to their present place by a rope. This is Berezkin's fished-out earth (B4), a cousin of the earth-diver, and it is the counter-case: the reason Mythoscope is not merely a catalogue of things it cannot do.

With just 36 attesting traditions, B4 is far narrower than the swan-maiden or the sun-and-moon pairing. And unlike the magic wife, its carriers are not scattered across the language tree. They cluster, within the Austronesian world, in a way the tree-reading method registers as descent rather than diffusion — and that single fact unlocks everything the machine can otherwise only wish for.

Placed in time and space

Because B4 behaves like an inheritance, the gated method knows it may be dated by clade depth rather than left undated as the areal motifs must be. Reconstructing its origin places its centroid in Western Oceania, with a ceiling at the Austronesian expansion of about 5,200 years ago. Mapping its spread draws lines fanning out across the Pacific along the paths of that expansion.

Drawing this correctly takes one small cartographic care the machine applies automatically: the map is re-centred on the motif's own region, its central meridian set to the circular mean of its traditions, so that a Pacific-spread distribution reads as a coherent fan around the ocean rather than being torn in two by the arbitrary Atlantic seam of a standard projection.

Here, for once, the fossil-record dream of comparative mythology is realised: not just that a motif was inherited, but roughly when, from where, and along which routes it travelled.

Honest about resolution

The estimate does not overclaim. It is a family-level point estimate, not a node-consistent reconstruction with proper uncertainty; a dated tree with full Bayesian phylogeography would sharpen it. But even at family resolution, B4 is a motif the machine genuinely cracks. It stands for the roughly one percent of the corpus that behaves like the fossil the whole enterprise was built to read.

Three motifs, three verdicts. The swan-maiden: broad, areal, undatable from its distribution. Sun and moon: deep, real, and yet a witness to the wall past which distribution cannot see. The fished-up earth: inherited — and therefore placed in time and space.

See how these three sit together in What we found, and how B4's Austronesian cousins map across catalogues in The crosswalk.
