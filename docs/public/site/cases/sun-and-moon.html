Sun and Moon as Kin: The Deep Substrate, and the Wall

Across an astonishing range of unrelated traditions, the sun is imagined as male and the moon as female. This is Berezkin's male sun and female moon (A3) — the moon is female or bisexual, the sun is male — a gendered celestial pairing attested in 362 traditions. It is cosmological to its core: the night sky is available to everyone, and the pairing is elementary.

A deep substrate that survives the controls

A3 is a leading candidate for the deep celestial substrate that Mythoscope confirmed is real. Its pan-global, cross-hemispheric distribution survives the coverage and banality controls that strip the inflated antiquity off lesser motifs — the checks designed to catch a motif that merely looks old because it is common or well-sampled. On the depth score it reads as deep; in the gated combination it files as areal-deep; and its content sits squarely in Category A, the cosmological pole that the theme cross-check associated with the deepest layer.

Everything about A3 says ancient.

The wall

And yet. Everything about the swan-maiden says travelled — and the mixture model gives the two motifs the same decomposition, a descent component of about 0.16 apiece.

Placed side by side, the pan-global cosmological pairing that may be tens of millennia old and the fairy-tale of the captured bird-bride that may be only a few thousand years young are, to every instrument the distribution affords, indistinguishable. This is the irreducible limit made vivid: not a weakness in one method, but a wall the evidence itself puts up. A deep shared inheritance and a wide diffusion cast the same shadow — broad, disjunct, areally scattered — and no score computed from that shadow can separate them.

Sun-and-moon and the swan-maiden are the project's two witnesses to the same fact: past a certain point, where a motif is found has told us everything it can, and the rest must come from outside the distribution — from genetics, corridors, and dated trees.

The machine confirms the deep substrate is real. It concedes, in the same breath, that it cannot date it. Where it stops, it stops honestly.

See the counter-case the machine does crack in The fished-up earth, and the whole pattern in What we found. The catalogue correspondences behind these motifs live in The crosswalk.
