# 10 · Discussion and outlook

*Computational Comparative Mythology: A Natural History of the Motif — Chapter 10 of 10. Draft.*

---

The three case studies of Chapter 9 showed the machine working on motifs a reader can hold in mind; this
closing chapter draws the whole argument together and ends where an honest programme should — on the
frontier rather than on a restated conclusion. Three findings cut across everything, one large negative
result organises the future, and a short list of concrete milestones would carry the programme forward.

## 10.1 Three conclusions

**Geography is primary; descent is a minority mode; deep inheritance is real but small.** The dominant
signal in a global motif corpus is areal — isolation by distance and neighbour diffusion. Against that
background stands a well-defined descent minority, on the order of one motif in a hundred, that tracks
language and can be dated to the Eurasian fairy-tale belt around 5,500 years ago; and beneath both, a
small trans-hemispheric substrate, chiefly celestial cosmology, that survives every de-confounding
control and pools where humans have lived longest. This picture is consistent with, and quantifies,
both the phylogenetic programme that finds descent in Indo-European tales and the Pleistocene-substrate
hypotheses that posit a deep global layer — while showing that the two operate on largely disjoint
slices of the corpus. Neither camp was wrong; each described a different one percent.

**Depth is a property of distribution, not of meaning, and it is only partly recoverable.** No linear
score, no embedding, and no single facet dates a motif. The diagnostic contrast between a deep shared
substrate and a widely diffused innovation — the sun-and-moon siblings against the swan-maiden —
is irreducible from distribution alone and demands external calibration. Honest connectivity modelling
is what keeps this from being an excuse for over-fitting: a coarse landscape-friction model *failed* a
gate set for it in advance, and only dated historical corridors added real cross-area signal. The
discipline of the falsifiable gate — of reporting the clean negatives — is the main thing standing
between a programme like this and a compelling story fitted to noise.

**A catalogue's theme axis encodes a choice, and there are two good ones.** The traditional taxonomy
orders motifs by what they explain; a bottom-up taxonomy orders them by how the story is built. Neither
is correct, because they are orthogonal projections onto different questions, and each is better at a
different job — narrative form for describing a tradition and for recovering the depth hidden inside
genre catch-alls, etiological function for reading geography. The practical implication is a two-facet
representation, not a replacement.

## 10.2 The residual as the map of future work

The single largest fact the corpus yields is not any of the positive findings but the size of what they
leave unexplained. A tradition's describable facets recover only about a third of motif similarity; the
connectivity channels of Chapter 7 closed only a sliver of the remaining two-thirds; and the deep-versus-
diffuse ambiguity cannot be resolved from distribution at all. This **convergence residual** is not a
failure of the programme — it is the programme's agenda, drawn as a map (Figure 10.1). Its shape names
precisely what is missing: not more distributional cleverness, which has been pushed about as far as the
data allows, but *external* evidence of the channels along which motifs actually moved and the lineages
along which they were actually inherited. Each region of the residual points to a specific join that
would light it up. Reading the residual this way turns a two-thirds shortfall from an embarrassment into
a research design.

## 10.3 Open milestones, and release

Four milestones would carry the programme across that frontier.

The first is **finishing motif induction from raw text** — the principal open goal, and the purpose the
main infrastructure was built to serve. What remains is not architecture, which exists, but validated
output at scale: induced motifs checked against the curated gold standard, the field's stubborn simple
baselines actually beaten, and culture-bearers consulted where the meaning of a motif cannot be read
off the text. This is the milestone that would let the programme grow its own corpus rather than inherit
one.

The second is **closing the convergence residual** with the external joins the residual names: fine-grained
genetic data for a true third axis independent of both language and geography, wired trade-route networks
to test contact channels the coarse landscape model missed, and node-level Bayesian dating on a proper
dated tree to replace the current family-resolution ages. These are the expensive joins, and they are
expensive precisely because they are the ones that would move the needle.

The third is **productionising the two-facet theme taxonomy** — the etiological and the narrative axes
together — into the pipeline as standing infrastructure rather than a prototype result, so that every
downstream analysis can use both.

The fourth is **release**: the corpus, the cross-index links, the tools, and the derived data — including
the narrative-taxonomy facet — published openly, with provenance, so that the whole apparatus can be
reused, checked, and extended by others. The point of building the machine in the open is that the
residual is a shared problem, and shared problems are solved faster in the open.

---

The natural history of the motif, then, ends as natural history usually does at the close of its first
systematic pass: with a real order established, a datable minority resolved, a deep spine confirmed and
measured, and a large, honestly bounded residual that names the next joins to make. The geography of a
myth is written primarily in where it is attested, not in what it says; a tradition is best described by
the narrative form of its corpus, while the classical themes remain the better reader of its geography;
and the difference between deep inheritance and wide diffusion is, for now, the boundary that external
calibration must cross. That boundary is not the programme's failure. It is its map of what comes next.
