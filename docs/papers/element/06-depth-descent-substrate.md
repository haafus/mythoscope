# 6 · Depth, descent, and the deep substrate

*Computational Comparative Mythology: A Natural History of the Motif — Chapter 6 of 10. Draft.*

---

This is the chapter the rest of the book is built to support. Chapter 5 established that the corpus
carries real structure and measured how far a tradition's describable facets fall short of explaining
it. Now the question is the one comparative mythology has always wanted answered: given a motif's
distribution across the world, **how old is it, and how did it get where it is?** The answer this
chapter reaches has four parts. Areal diffusion is the dominant mode. A small descent minority tracks
language and can be dated. A faint deep substrate is real but small. And the difference between a deep
inheritance and a wide diffusion is, past a certain point, irreducible from distribution alone. Each of
these is a de-confounded finding, and the last is a negative result the programme reports as carefully
as the positive ones.

## 6.1 Depth from the shape of a distribution

The first estimator reads a motif's time-depth directly off the geometry of its footprint. Chapter 3's
premises say how: not raw prevalence — a motif can be common because it is old *or* because it is
catchy and recently borrowed everywhere — but the *shape* of the spread, and above all its
**disjunction.** A motif attested widely but contiguously, spreading across an unbroken swath of
neighbouring peoples, is the signature of contact, which manufactures broad contiguous ranges quickly.
A motif attested on both sides of an ocean, in continental sets that have been separated since the
peopling of the world, is much harder to explain by recent borrowing. The depth score therefore
combines prevalence, geographic spread, spatial fragmentation, language-family span, and — weighted
most heavily — the number of separate continental "mega-sets" a motif touches, orienting every feature
toward antiquity by the disjunction-outranks-breadth rule (Figure 6.1). The score is a hypothesis with
a confidence, never a recorded fact, exactly as the given/inferred discipline of Chapter 3 requires;
and it carries a limitation that must be stated with it, because a breadth-and-disjunction proxy
inevitably conflates ancient descent with wide diffusion — the very ambiguity §6.5 will show to be
irreducible.

## 6.2 Depth from the language tree

The second estimator asks a different question of the same motif: not what shape its footprint has, but
whether that footprint is **clustered on the tree of languages.** If a motif is inherited down a
lineage, the traditions that carry it should sit together on the language-classification tree, as a
heritable character does on a phylogeny; if it spreads by contact, its carriers should be scattered
across the tree regardless of descent. Placing each motif on the tree and running ancestral-state
reconstruction — Fitch parsimony, upgraded to a two-state gain/loss model with marginal reconstruction
— recovers, for every motif, an inferred history of gains and losses along the branches. The decisive
quantity is the **phylogenetic signal**: how clustered the real distribution is compared with the same
number of carriers shuffled at random across the tree (Figure 6.2). A motif whose observed clustering
far exceeds the permuted null is behaving like an inheritance; one whose clustering is
indistinguishable from random scatter is behaving like a diffusion. Crucially, theme is kept *out* of
this estimation entirely, so that a motif's subject can serve later as an independent check rather than
a circular input. Method A reads the map; Method B reads the tree; and the two are complementary rather
than redundant, because the tree separates the *mode* — descent or diffusion — that the map alone
cannot.

## 6.3 Areal diffusion dominates, and the descent minority is small

Putting the two estimators together produces the chapter's first headline, and it is a deflationary
one for anyone hoping the corpus is mostly a fossil record of inheritance. When motifs are placed on
the tree, **only about one percent are simultaneously broad and clade-clustered** — broad enough to
matter and clustered enough to be descent rather than diffusion. And that one percent is not random: it
is the Eurasian fairy-tale core — Cinderella, the "seven at a blow" tale of the boastful
weakling — motifs that track the branching of language families within Eurasia. This is an independent
recovery, from a whole areal catalogue rather than a hand-picked tale-type set, of the published
finding that märchen follow language phylogeny within the Indo-European world. The corpus confirms it
without being tuned to.

Everything else broad is broad by *diffusion*. Cosmology, the trickster, and the swan-maiden are all
widespread, but their distributions are areally scattered, not clade-clustered. The gated combination
of the two methods lets Method B pick the mode and the mode pick the dating instrument, and it splits
the otherwise-unresolvable "broad" motifs three ways: **areal-deep**, **descent**, and **areal-broad**
(Figure 6.3). Here the theme cross-check pays off exactly as Chapter 3 promised it might. Theme was
never fed to either estimator, yet the Category-A cosmological share of a motif's content falls from
64% in the deep-areal mode to 24% in the descent mode — the descent minority is disproportionately
*narrative* (fairy tales), the deep-areal material disproportionately *cosmological*, and the model
recovered that split without ever being told what any motif was about. An assumption held out of the
estimation, corroborating the estimation, is the strongest kind of internal evidence the programme can
offer.

## 6.4 The deep substrate is real, but small

The most contested claim in comparative mythology is that beneath the areal churn lies a deep,
trans-hemispheric substrate — a stock of motifs, chiefly celestial cosmology, old enough to predate the
peopling of the continents. The corpus supports a version of this claim, but a chastened one, and only
after the two mandatory controls of Chapter 3 are applied.

The first control is coverage weighting, which shrinks apparent breadth by about 31% once the
densely-catalogued traditions stop inflating every motif's range. The second is a banality proxy that
flags motifs simple enough to have been reinvented independently rather than transmitted. Under both
together, 504 motifs — about 15% — change mode, mostly sliding from areal-broad to areal-recent as
their inflated ranges deflate (Figure 6.5). This is the sound of a great deal of apparent antiquity
evaporating. And yet the deep spine does not vanish: of the 480 motifs that touch both hemispheres, 320
survive both controls. The deep, trans-hemispheric class is real. It is simply *small* — a spine, not
a skeleton — and its reality is established precisely by the fact that it is what remains after the
controls have taken their share.

That the survivors are not a coding artefact of one catalogue is confirmed by cross-catalogue
replication. Just under half of all motifs — 48% — are corroborated by an independent index through the
crosswalk, and the corroboration is **theme-blind**: cosmological motifs are corroborated at 49%,
tale-types at 49%, so the replication is not quietly favouring one kind of material over another. It is
also higher for exactly the motifs the deep-substrate claim rests on — 54% for broad motifs against 20%
for narrow ones — so the analysis leans on a replicated core rather than on Berezkin's coding alone.
The deep substrate is small, real, and independently attested.

## 6.5 The irreducible limit, and why content cannot break it

Now the negative result, which the programme regards as one of its central contributions rather than a
disappointment. A continuous three-way mixture model — decomposing each motif into descent, areal, and
reinvention components — confirms the overall picture: most motifs are areal-dominant (2,311 of 2,775),
and Category B is very slightly more inheritable than Category A. But it also delivers a verdict on the
two motifs the book uses as diagnostics. The sun and moon imagined as kin (catalogued A3), a prime
candidate for the deepest cosmological substrate, and the swan-maiden (K25), a candidate for a much
later fairy-tale spread, receive **near-identical mixtures** — a descent component of about 0.16
apiece (Figure 6.4). The model cannot tell them apart. One may be a hundred-thousand-year-old
inheritance and the other a few-thousand-year-old diffusion, and from their distributions alone they
look the same.

This is not a failure of a particular method; it is a property of the evidence. A deep shared
substrate and a widely diffused innovation produce the same kind of footprint — broad, disjunct,
areally scattered — and no score computed from that footprint can separate them. Nor can *content*
supply the missing signal, which one might hope it could. Nearest-by-meaning motifs do share a theme
group 58% of the time, against a 20% chance baseline, so meaning is far from random — but meaning
barely predicts *age*: content correlates only about r ≈ 0.28 with breadth and 0.18 with prevalence
(Figure 6.6). **What a motif is about is not how old it is.** No embedding, no linear score, and no
single facet dates a motif, and the diagnostic contrast between A3 and K25 is irreducible from
distribution alone. Separating them requires stepping outside the distribution entirely — to genetics,
to historical corridors, to node-level dated phylogenies — which is the boundary Chapter 7 begins to
probe and Chapter 10 hands to future work. The residual is not tuned away; it is named.

## 6.6 Dating the descent minority

Where the mode *can* be resolved — the descent minority Method B isolates — the corpus can be pushed
one step further, from ordinal depth to calendar age. Joining the traditions to the Glottolog language
catalogue and calibrating clade depth against published family-expansion dates yields calendar ages for
the **451** descent motifs, concentrated, as expected, at the Indo-European märchen belt around 5,500
years ago (Figure 6.7). The areal majority — A3, K25 and their kind — is correctly left *undated*,
because dating an areally diffused motif by clade depth would be meaningless, and the gated method knows
not to try.

The same machinery can place a descent motif in space as well as time. Reconstructing the origin of the
fished-up-earth motif (B4) — the earth-diver's cousin, in which land is hauled up from the sea — puts
its centroid in Western Oceania with a ceiling at the Austronesian expansion of about 5,200 years ago,
and maps its spread as lines fanning out across the Pacific. These are family-resolution point
estimates, not node-consistent reconstructions with proper uncertainty; a dated tree and full Bayesian
phylogeography would sharpen them, and that is left as future work. But even at family resolution, the
descent minority is the one part of the corpus where the programme can say not just *that* a motif was
inherited but roughly *when* and *from where* — the fossil-record dream, realised for the one percent of
the corpus that actually behaves like a fossil.

## 6.7 Tradition stratigraphy: the falsification test

The chapter's estimators can be turned around to provide a genuine test that could have failed. If the
depth scores mean anything, then traditions rich in deep-substrate motifs ought to sit in the regions
peopled earliest — deep myth should pool where humans have been longest. This is a prediction, and it
is falsifiable: run it, and the deep-share of a tradition either does or does not track its region's
first-peopling age.

It does. The partial correlation between a tradition's deep-substrate share and the antiquity of its
region's settlement, controlling for coverage, is **+0.48** (Figure 6.8). The gradient runs in the
right order and at plausible magnitudes: Sub-Saharan Africa highest at 63% deep-share against a
settlement age near 65,000 years, the early Old World in the middle at 56–59%, and the Americas lowest
at 48–49% against a settlement age near 14,000 years. What makes this more than a restatement of the
depth score is what coverage does to it: a tradition's deep-share is *negatively* correlated (−0.30)
with how thoroughly it was catalogued, so the sampling confound was **masking** the signal, not faking
it. Controlling for coverage *strengthens* the correlation rather than weakening it — the opposite of
the theme-by-area finding in Chapter 5, and a reminder that de-confounding is not a machine for
shrinking effects but for finding their true size. A programme that reported only raw numbers would
have understated this result; one that reported only its successes would never have set the test at
all.

---

The phylogeny stage thus resolves what it can and marks what it cannot. Areal diffusion is the dominant
mode; a datable one-percent descent minority tracks language and can be placed in time and space; a
small trans-hemispheric substrate is real, survives every control, and pools where humans have lived
longest. And against all of this stands the irreducible limit — the point past which a deep inheritance
and a wide diffusion cannot be told apart from distribution alone. That limit is the largest single
fact the corpus yields, because it defines the remaining work. The next chapter asks whether the
convergence residual it leaves behind can be closed by modelling the physical and historical channels
along which motifs actually moved.
