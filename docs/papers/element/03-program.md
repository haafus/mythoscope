# 3 · A natural-history program

*Computational Comparative Mythology: A Natural History of the Motif — Chapter 3 of 10. Draft.*

---

Chapter 2 left the field with strong tools and a weak spine: powerful ways to represent and cluster
narrative, but a habit of running one-off correlations over a motif catalogue treated as a flat pool,
with no explicit separation of what is observed from what is inferred and no discipline of prediction
that could be wrong. This chapter supplies the spine. It is deliberately methodological and comes
before any result, because everything in Chapters 5 through 9 is produced under the commitments set
out here, and a reader who has them in hand can hold each later claim to the standard the programme
sets for itself.

## 3.1 The arc: collect, describe, classify, explain

The organising idea is simple and old: **comparative mythology is a natural-history science, and its
data is a catalogue.** Biology moved from cabinets of specimens to comparative anatomy to Linnaean
systematics to phylogeny; historical linguistics moved from wordlists to grammars to language families
to reconstruction. A catalogue of some three and a half thousand motifs distributed across a thousand
traditions is the same kind of object, and it rewards the same arc — **collect, describe, classify,
explain** (Figure 3.1).

> **Figure 3.1.** The natural-history arc as a dependency order: each stage presupposes the one before —
> you cannot classify what you have not described, or explain a distribution you have not mapped.

The crucial point about the arc is that it is a **dependency order, not a schedule.** The stages are
not four phases of a project to be worked through and ticked off; they are a logical ordering in which
each stage presupposes the one before. You cannot classify what you have not described, and you cannot
explain a distribution you have not first mapped. This is why, in what follows, systematics must
precede phylogeny — not out of a taste for tidiness, but because a claim about *how* a distribution
arose is meaningless until the distribution itself has been established and shown to be real rather
than an artefact of collecting. When the order is violated — when a striking explanatory story is told
about a pattern that has not been de-confounded — the result is the kind of over-reach the field has
learned to distrust.

Spelled out a little more finely, the arc has five joints. **Collection and curation** turns raw,
uneven sources into a clean, provenanced record of which traditions carry which motifs — and, its
defining move, carries the *bias* of collection forward rather than forgetting it. **Description**, the
morphology stage, fixes what a single motif is and how it is measured: its definition, its identity
across the catalogues, its representation. **Systematics** asks what kinds there are and along which
axes traditions and motifs vary — the closed space of describable facets. **Phylogeny and etiology**
asks, of each mapped distribution, how it arose and how old it is. And **synthesis** asks which
regularities survive all the controls and what still resists them. The book walks this arc: Chapter 4
is collection and description, Chapter 5 is systematics, Chapter 6 is phylogeny and etiology, and
Chapters 7 through 10 are synthesis — including, crucially, the honest accounting of what does not
resolve.

The non-obvious weight sits on the first joint. In this corpus the number of motifs recorded for a
tradition ranges from a single entry to more than seven hundred, with a median near seventy-five. A
tradition that has been catalogued densely will look "central" — connected, motif-rich, typical — for
reasons that have nothing to do with the antiquity or the history of its myths and everything to do
with how thoroughly someone wrote it down. If that single fact is not carried forward from stage one
into every later stage, the programme will keep rediscovering the shape of the collecting enterprise
and mistaking it for the shape of myth. The attestation-intensity control introduced here is therefore
not a local fix; it is a thread that runs through the entire book.

## 3.2 The three-entity model, and the one inferred field

Description requires a data model, and the right one turns on a distinction the field has often
blurred. **Time-depth is a property of a motif, not of a tradition.** Any living tradition carries
motifs of many different ages at once — a Sub-Saharan corpus may hold both a deep African-substratum
motif and a recently arrived Islamic one — so it is a category error to speak of a tradition as
"old." What is old, or young, or deep, or shallow, is a *motif's distribution*. Recognising this
yields three entities, each with a deliberately small vocabulary (Figure 3.2).

> **Figure 3.2.** The three-entity data model — tradition, motif, attestation — with the given /
> deterministic facets marked apart from the single inferred field (`stratum`), the one quantity that is
> always a hypothesis carrying a confidence rather than an observed fact.

A **tradition** is described by a handful of *given or deterministic* facets: its macro-area (one of
twelve), its language or religious family, its subsistence economy (one of four), the thematic
composition of its corpus, its coordinates, and its attestation richness. A **motif** is described by
its definition, its links to counterparts in the other catalogues, its theme, and — the single
exception — its `stratum`: its time-depth and mode of spread. An **attestation** is the bare fact that
a given motif is present in a given tradition; it is the observed raw material from which everything
else is computed.

The model's discipline lies in one line drawn through it: **separate the given from the inferred, and
attach a confidence to the inferred.** Attestation is *observed*. A tradition's facets are *given* by
external data or *deterministic* functions of it. Only one field in the whole model is *inferred* — a
motif's stratum — and it is therefore never a fact but always a hypothesis carrying a confidence. Most
failures in quantitative folkloristics, on this view, come from letting an inferred quantity
masquerade as data: a depth score gets computed, then treated as if it were a recorded property, and
its uncertainty quietly evaporates as it is passed downstream. Holding the line — one inferred field,
always hypothesised, never observed — is what keeps the eventual conclusions honest about how much they
actually know.

Expressiveness in the model is **multiplicative and cross-entity** rather than concentrated in any one
fine-grained axis. A tradition's profile is the product of its area, family, and subsistence; each
attested motif carries a theme and a stratum. Following Berezkin's own methodological injunction that
the catalogue must be analysed *in parts, primarily by thematic group*, and never as a single
undifferentiated pool, the analyses fix a thematic slice first and cross-cut it by the tradition axes
within that slice. No single axis has to be finely resolved, because the resolution comes from the
combination. (Whether the chosen facets are in fact the right and non-redundant ones is not assumed
but audited, in Chapter 5; the audit revises the model, which is the point of building it explicitly.)

## 3.3 Assumptions, stated so they can be attacked

A programme that infers time-depth from distribution rests on premises, and the programme's second
commitment is to **state those premises once, in the open, so that each can be attacked on its own**
rather than smuggled in as background common sense. They come in two kinds.

The **substantive hypotheses** are empirical, falsifiable claims about how folklore actually behaves.
The load-bearing one is that *the distribution of a motif carries information about its age* at all —
that where and how widely a motif is found is not independent of how old it is. On top of it sit
claims that a shared motif spreads either by descent down a lineage or by contact between neighbours,
often as a mixture; that great breadth *combined with* spread across separate language families and,
above all, across geographic barriers — cross-continental **disjunction** — points to antiquity; that
disjunction outranks mere breadth, because contiguous spread is something contact manufactures quickly
whereas a motif found on both sides of an ocean is harder to explain by recent borrowing; that
clustering on the language tree signals descent while scatter signals areal diffusion; and that clade
depth dates a descent motif while geographic span, read against the archaeological sequence in which
the continents were peopled, dates an areal one.

The **methodological axioms** are not claims about the world but rules imposed to keep the numbers
honest: keep theme *out* of the estimation of stratum, so that theme can serve as an independent
cross-check rather than a circular input; carry the attestation-intensity control at every stage;
test for spatial autocorrelation — Galton's problem — rather than assuming independence; and
corroborate findings across the independent catalogues rather than trusting any single one's coding.

Writing the premises down this way has already paid off, because several have since been tested and
some revised. The assumption that the tradition facets would be roughly orthogonal was *falsified* —
area and family turn out to be strongly entangled, tracking one shared history of peopling. A proposed
upgrade from simple distance to physical landscape connectivity *failed a test set in advance*, with
plain great-circle distance winning out of sample. The comfortable reading that an African substratum
must be the oldest layer was *weakened* by a confirmed signal of back-migration into Africa. And the
theme cross-check *corroborated* the depth estimates independently, with the cosmological share of a
motif's content falling sharply from the deep-areal mode to the descent mode exactly as the model
predicted. An assumption that survives an attack becomes evidence; one that fails redirects the
programme. Either way the field is better off than it is when its premises are left unstated — which is
the whole reason for stating them.

## 3.4 De-confounding and falsifiable gates

Two further commitments turn the stance into a method that can be trusted, and both are refusals as
much as techniques.

The first is that **de-confounding is not optional.** The attestation-intensity of a tradition is
treated, throughout, in whatever form each analysis requires: as a weight that lets every tradition
contribute comparably instead of letting the densely catalogued corpora dominate; as a correction
built into the models of who-carries-what; and as an exposure term in the joint factorisation of the
whole matrix. A separate proxy flags motifs that are "banal" enough to have been reinvented rather
than transmitted. Spatial autocorrelation is controlled by shuffling labels only within strata, so
that a neighbourhood's structure is held fixed and an association has to survive being tested against
the right null. And every headline is carried in two forms — raw and de-confounded — so that a reader
can see for themselves exactly how much of a pattern the sampling explains. A result that appears only
in the raw version and dissolves under the controls is not a finding; it is a description of the
archive.

The second is the discipline of the **falsifiable gate.** A candidate improvement to the method is not
adopted because it is theoretically attractive or because it fits the story; it must **beat the
incumbent out of sample** before it earns its place, and when it fails, the clean negative is reported
rather than buried. This is what happened to the landscape-connectivity model in Chapter 7, and the
episode is worth the space precisely because the answer was *no*. A field that reports only its
successes cannot be trusted with a question as seductive as the origin of myth, because there will
always be some transformation of the data under which a compelling pattern appears. The willingness to
set a gate in advance and to walk away when the data does not clear it is the main thing standing
between this programme and an elegant over-fit.

These commitments have a limit, and the programme states it up front rather than discovering it with
embarrassment later. **From distribution alone, a deep shared inheritance and a widely diffused
innovation can be genuinely indistinguishable.** The two diagnostic motifs the book returns to — the
sun and moon imagined as kin, and the swan-maiden — receive nearly identical decompositions into
descent and diffusion, even though one is a candidate for the deepest cosmological substrate and the
other for a much later fairy-tale spread. No score, no embedding, and no amount of care with the
existing data separates them. This irreducibility is not a defect to be tuned away; it is a boundary,
and naming it correctly is what tells us which *external* evidence — genetics, historical trade
corridors, node-level dated phylogenies — would have to be brought in to cross it. The residual that
the method cannot explain is, in the end, not the programme's failure but its agenda; the rest of the
book is an attempt to measure that residual honestly and to say precisely where it begins.
