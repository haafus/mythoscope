# World Mythology & Religion Reference Works — A Survey

*Internal reference document. Every factual claim is followed by or grouped under a source URL; a consolidated Sources list appears at the end. Compiled July 2026.*

## Scope and method

This survey covers the major encyclopedias, dictionaries, handbooks, and digital databases that attempt worldwide (or comparatively broad) coverage of mythology and comparative religion, in both English and other scholarly languages (Russian, French, German). For each work it records bibliographic facts, scholarly standing, organizing principle, how it partitions world mythology, and its theoretical stance. A cross-cutting synthesis then compares how these works "carve the world" and why some traditions get dedicated sections while others are pooled.

## Part I — Per-Work Profiles

### 1. New Larousse Encyclopedia of Mythology (Guirand / Graves)

- **Full title:** *New Larousse Encyclopedia of Mythology* (originally *Larousse Encyclopedia of Mythology*; French original *Larousse mythologie générale*, ed. Félix Guirand).
- **Editor / contributors:** Félix Guirand (editor); introduction by Robert Graves; translated by Richard Aldington and Delano Ames.
- **Publisher / editions:** First English edition 1959 (as *Larousse Encyclopedia of Mythology*); "New" edition 1968; further printings incl. 1987. English-language publishers include Prometheus Press / Hamlyn / Crescent.
- **Format:** Single large illustrated volume (~500 pp.), heavily illustrated — long a popular gateway reference. https://archive.org/details/newlarousseencyc0000unse_l4n9 , https://www.amazon.com/Larousse-Encyclopedia-Mythology-Felix-Guirand/dp/0517004046
- **Organizing principle:** **Thematic essays organized by region/culture** (not an A–Z dictionary). Chapters are narrative surveys written by contributors, each covering one cultural tradition, with subsections by pantheon/theme.
- **Sections (top-level chapter divisions):** Introduction; Prehistoric mythology; Egyptian; Assyro-Babylonian; Phoenician; Greek; Roman; Celtic; Teutonic; Slavonic; Finno-Ugric; Ancient Persia; Indian; Chinese; Japanese; Mythology of the Two Americas; Oceanic; Mythology of Black Africa (~17–18 sections). Subsections within, e.g., Greek: "Prehellenic mythology," "The mythology of classical Greece," "The heroes"; Indian: "The Brahmanic Dharma," "The Heretical Dharmas," "Mythology of Hinduism." https://archive.org/details/newlarousseencyc0000unse_l4n9
- **Authority / stance:** Popular-scholarly rather than peer-reviewed; French-tradition, descriptive/narrative, strong on the classical Mediterranean and Europe. Its bias is the classic one: fine-grained for literate Old World traditions, coarse pooling of "the Two Americas," "Oceanic," and "Black Africa" into single chapters. Widely reprinted and influential as a general-audience standard.

### 2. The Mythology of All Races (Gray / MacCulloch, 13 vols)

- **Full title:** *The Mythology of All Races*, in thirteen volumes.
- **Editors:** Louis Herbert Gray (editor); John Arnott MacCulloch (co-editor for later volumes); George Foot Moore (consulting editor).
- **Publisher / dates:** Marshall Jones Company, Boston, 1916–1932. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mythology_of_All_Races
- **Structure:** **Region/culture per volume**, each volume a monograph by a specialist. Volume list:
  1. Greek and Roman — W. S. Fox (1916)
  2. Eddic — J. A. MacCulloch (1930)
  3. Celtic and Slavic — MacCulloch & Jan Máchal (1918)
  4. Finno-Ugric, Siberian — Uno Holmberg (1927)
  5. Semitic — S. H. Langdon (1931)
  6. Indian and Iranian — A. B. Keith & Albert Carnoy (1917)
  7. Armenian and African — M. H. Ananikian & Alice Werner (1925)
  8. Chinese and Japanese — J. C. Ferguson & Masaharu Anesaki (1928)
  9. Oceanic — R. B. Dixon (1916)
  10. North American — H. B. Alexander (1916)
  11. Latin American — H. B. Alexander (1920)
  12. Egyptian and Indo-Chinese — W. M. Müller & J. G. Scott (1918)
  13. Complete Index (1932). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mythology_of_All_Races , https://archive.org/details/mythologyofallra0002unse
- **Authority / stance:** Early-20th-century scholarly monograph series, each volume by a recognized academic specialist (Keith on India, Langdon on Semitic, Anesaki on Japan). Historical-philological approach. Now dated (esp. terminology and race-era framing, "Indo-Chinese," "African" lumping) but still cited for depth per tradition. Note the coarse pooling again: two whole continents (N. America, Latin America) get one author each; "Oceanic" and "African" each single volumes/sections.

### 3. Encyclopedia of Religion (Eliade; 2nd ed. Lindsay Jones) — Macmillan

- **Full title:** *The Encyclopedia of Religion.*
- **Editor:** 1st ed. Mircea Eliade (editor-in-chief); 2nd ed. Lindsay Jones (Ohio State University).
- **Publisher / editions:** 1st ed. Macmillan, 1987 — **16 volumes**, ~5 million words. 2nd ed. Macmillan Reference USA / Thomson Gale, 2005 — **15 volumes**. https://www.abebooks.com/9780029094808/Encyclopedia-Religion-16-Volume-Set-0029094801/plp , https://search.worldcat.org/title/oclc/56057973
- **Entries:** 2nd ed. retains most of the ~2,750 original entries (many revised) plus ~600 new articles — roughly **3,300 entries**, each with bibliography. https://www.betterworldbooks.com/product/detail/encyclopedia-of-religion-9780028657332
- **Organizing principle:** **Alphabetical A–Z dictionary/encyclopedia** of religions, deities, concepts, thinkers, rituals, and methods — comparative religion rather than mythology per se, but the single most authoritative cross-cultural reference for myth-in-religion.
- **Authority / stance:** The flagship academic reference in religious studies; peer-reviewed, authored by leading scholars. Eliade's editorial vision was **phenomenological and non-reductionist** (the "sacred" as an irreducible category), comparative and cross-cultural. **Criticisms:** methodologically uncritical universalism; sweeping generalizations; an alleged Western/Christian and "antihistorical" bias in positing a universal "sacred." The 2nd edition deliberately added historicist and social-scientific correctives. https://www.britannica.com/topic/phenomenology-of-religion , https://www.academia.edu/3622682/

### 4. Man, Myth & Magic (Richard Cavendish)

- **Full title:** *Man, Myth & Magic: An Illustrated Encyclopedia of the Supernatural* (later subtitle *...of Mythology, Religion and the Unknown*).
- **Editor:** Richard Cavendish (1930–2016); art direction Brian Innes.
- **Publisher / editions:** BPC Publishing, launched 1970 as a British weekly **partwork — 112 issues, ~1,000 articles, ~5,000 illustrations**; bound as a 24-volume set (1970); Marshall Cavendish 21-volume revised edition (1995); Cavendish Square 5-volume library set (2014). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man,_Myth_%26_Magic_(encyclopedia)
- **Organizing principle:** **Alphabetical A–Z** across the run (later reissues regroup thematically).
- **Coverage:** Magic, mythology, religion, folklore, occult, the supernatural — broad and popular in tone.
- **Authority / stance:** Popular-but-credentialed: over 200 academic contributors, including **E. R. Dodds, Mircea Eliade, Glyn Daniel, C. A. Burland**. Accessible style; strongest as a mid-century survey of Western esotericism, folklore, and the supernatural rather than a rigorous myth reference. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man,_Myth_%26_Magic_(encyclopedia)

### 5. Oxford & Routledge dictionaries of world / classical mythology

- **A Dictionary of World Mythology (Arthur Cotterell, OUP):** first published 1979/1986, Oxford Paperback Reference 1997. **Organizing principle unusual — region-grouped rather than strictly A–Z:** Cotterell divides world myth into **seven sections — West Asia, South & Central Asia, East Asia, Europe, America, Africa, Oceania** — with entries inside each. Single-author, concise, general-audience. https://global.oup.com/academic/product/a-dictionary-of-world-mythology-9780192177476 , https://archive.org/details/dictionaryofworl00cott_0
- **The Oxford Dictionary of Classical Myth and Religion (Simon Price & Emily Kearns, 2003):** drawn from the *Oxford Classical Dictionary*; A–Z; covers Greek and Roman myth, religion, cult places, texts, divination, astrology, magic. Peer-reviewed, authoritative for the classical world only. https://www.amazon.com/Dictionary-Classical-Religion-Paperback-Reference/dp/0192802895
- **Oxford Classical Dictionary (OCD), and its online form (oxfordre.com/classics):** the standard academic reference for Greco-Roman antiquity; long "mythology" survey article. https://oxfordre.com/classics/display/10.1093/acrefore/9780199381135.001.0001/acrefore-9780199381135-e-4320
- **Routledge Dictionaries:** *The Routledge Dictionary of Gods and Goddesses, Devils and Demons* (Manfred Lurker) — **A–Z, over 1,800 deities/demons worldwide**; plus region-specific volumes, e.g. *A Dictionary of Ancient Near Eastern Mythology* (Gwendolyn Leick), *Routledge Dictionary of Egyptian Gods and Goddesses* (George Hart). https://www.routledge.com/The-Routledge-Dictionary-of-Gods-and-Goddesses-Devils-and-Demons/Lurker/p/book/9780415340182 , https://www.routledge.com/A-Dictionary-of-Ancient-Near-Eastern-Mythology/Leick/p/book/9780415198110

### 6. Funk & Wagnalls Standard Dictionary of Folklore, Mythology and Legend (Maria Leach)

- **Editor:** Maria Leach (editor), Jerome Fried (associate editor).
- **Publisher / editions:** Funk & Wagnalls; originally 2 vols. 1949–1950 (Vol. 1 A–I, Vol. 2 J–Z, ~1,196 pp.); one-volume reissue 1972 (~1,236 pp., marketed "3rd ed."); later HarperCollins reprint. https://archive.org/details/funkwagnallsstan00leac , https://www.si.edu/object/siris_sil_177104
- **Entries:** **~4,000 entries** in the standard set (some later unabridged/expanded figures cite up to ~8,000 articles across cultures). https://www.joyvspicer.com/joy-blog/2018/5/1/reference-book-funk-and-wagnalls-standard-dictionary-of-folklore-myth-and-legend-maria-leach-editor
- **Organizing principle:** **Alphabetical A–Z**, blending folklore, mythology, and legend — gods, heroes, fairies, demons, ballads, folk customs — across world cultures.
- **Authority / stance:** Landmark **folkloristic** reference (mid-20th c.); signed articles by folklorists and anthropologists. Comparative folklore emphasis rather than a pantheon-by-pantheon myth encyclopedia; still cited, though dated.

### 7. Encyclopædia Britannica — mythology coverage

- **Form:** Not a dedicated myth encyclopedia but the general encyclopedia's long survey article **"Myth"** (with sections "Approaches to the study of myth and mythology," "Functions of myth and mythology," etc.), plus per-tradition articles (Greek religion, Norse mythology, etc.). https://www.britannica.com/topic/myth/Approaches-to-the-study-of-myth-and-mythology
- **Authority / stance:** Signed scholarly overview articles with extensive bibliographies; the "Myth" article is a respected synthesis of theoretical approaches (including Eliade's). Thematic/analytical rather than a catalog of deities.

### 8. Мифы народов мира (Myths of the Peoples of the World) — S. A. Tokarev, ed.

- **Full title:** *Мифы народов мира. Энциклопедия* ("Myths of the Peoples of the World: Encyclopedia").
- **Chief editor:** S. A. Tokarev; deputy editor **E. M. Meletinsky**; major contributors include **Vyach. Vs. Ivanov, V. N. Toporov, I. M. Diakonov, A. F. Losev, S. S. Averintsev.**
- **Publisher / editions:** Soviet Encyclopedia (Советская энциклопедия), 1st ed. 1980–1982 in **2 volumes** (Vol. 1 A–K, 672 pp.; Vol. 2 K–Ya, 720 pp.); 2nd ed. 1987–1988; reprints 1991–1992, 1997, 2003; electronic ed. 2008. **>12,000 entries.** Awarded the **USSR State Prize (1990).** https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Мифы_народов_мира , https://archive.org/details/Myths_of_the_Peoples_of_the_World_Encyclopedia_Electronic_publication_Tokarev_and_others_2008
- **Organizing principle:** **Alphabetical A–Z encyclopedia** — but distinctive in that entries cover both individual mythological figures/motifs *and* large synthetic articles on the mythology of each people/region and on theoretical topics (myth, ritual, cosmogony, etc.).
- **Authority / stance:** Widely regarded as one of the most ambitious and rigorous single attempts at a **comprehensive, systematic account of world mythmaking**. Strong **structuralist and comparative** (Moscow–Tartu semiotic school: Ivanov, Toporov, Meletinsky) methodology, in tension with older evolutionist framing. Soviet-era ideological context is present but the scholarship is highly respected internationally. Notably broad and even coverage of non-literate and Eurasian traditions that Western works pool coarsely. https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Мифы_народов_мира , https://philologos.narod.ru/myth/mnmindex.htm

### 9. Dictionnaire des mythologies (Yves Bonnefoy) — French; and English *Mythologies*

- **Full title:** *Dictionnaire des mythologies et des religions des sociétés traditionnelles et du monde antique* (dir. Yves Bonnefoy), Flammarion, 2 vols., 1981.
- **English edition:** *Mythologies*, "a restructured translation," compiled by Bonnefoy, translated under the direction of **Wendy Doniger**, University of Chicago Press, 1991, 2 vols. (~1,267 pp.). **395 articles, ~100 contributors.** https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/M/bo3624533.html , https://www.amazon.com/Mythologies-2-Volumes-Yves-Bonnefoy/dp/0226064530
- **Organizing principle — a key contrast:** French original is **alphabetical**; the English *Mythologies* is **restructured geographically/culturally** into parts:
  - **Vol. 1:** Africa; The Ancient Near East; Celts, Norse, Slavs, Caucasians and Their Neighbors; Greece; Rome.
  - **Vol. 2:** Western Civilization in the Christian Era; South Asia, Iran, and Buddhism; Southeast Asia; East Asia and Inner Asia; The Americas and the South Pacific. https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/M/bo3624533.html
- **Authority / stance:** Elite academic, **deliberately non-exhaustive**: instead of cataloging every deity, it studies "major structures or problems" through essayistic articles by leading scholars — a **structuralist/problem-oriented, comparative** approach drawing on history of religions, anthropology, archaeology, linguistics. Also issued as thematic paperback selections (*Greek and Egyptian Mythologies*; *Roman and European Mythologies*; *Asian Mythologies*; *American, African, and Old European Mythologies*). https://www.persee.fr/doc/ahess_0395-2649_1982_num_37_5_282902_t1_0780_0000_001 , https://archive.org/details/greekegyptianmyt00bonn

### 10. Wörterbuch der Mythologie (H. W. Haussig) — German, multi-volume by region

- **Editors:** Hans Wilhelm Haussig and Egidius Schmalzriedt.
- **Publisher / dates:** Ernst Klett Verlag / Klett-Cotta, Stuttgart, from 1965 (individual volumes into the 1990s). https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wörterbuch_der_Mythologie , https://www.klett-cotta.de/buch/Andere_Handbuecher/Woerterbuch_der_Mythologie_Band_1/6710
- **Organizing principle:** **Regional volumes**, each subdivided into cultural sections, internally an alphabetical dictionary of names/motifs. Planned in three divisions (Abteilungen); **only Division I, "Die alten Kulturvölker" (The Ancient Cultured Peoples), was realized** — divisions on modern world religions and on sects/occult sciences were never published. The Greek-Roman volume (Band 3) was announced but **never appeared**, so the project is **incomplete.**
- **Published volumes (Division I):**
  - Bd. 1 — *Götter und Mythen im Vorderen Orient* (Ancient Near East: Mesopotamia, Anatolia/Hittites-Hurrites, Syria/Ugarit-Phoenicia, Egypt, Arabia, Ethiopia), 1965.
  - Bd. 2 — *Götter und Mythen im Alten Europa* (Germanic, Celtic, Slavic, Hungarian, Finnish, Baltic, Albanian, Basque, Berber, Iberian), 1973.
  - Bd. 3 — *Götter und Mythen der Griechen und Römer* — never published.
  - Bd. 4 — *Götter und Mythen der kaukasischen und iranischen Völker*, 1986.
  - Bd. 5 — *Götter und Mythen des indischen Subkontinents*, 1984.
  - Bd. 6 — *Götter und Mythen in Ostasien* (Shinto, Ainu, Taiwan minorities, Korean, Chinese Buddhist, Taoist), 1994.
  - Bd. 7/I–7/II — *Götter und Mythen in Zentralasien und Nordeurasien* (Manchu-Tungus, Turkic, Uralic/Siberian, Sami, Bon, Tibetan, Mongolian), 1998–1999. https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wörterbuch_der_Mythologie
- **Authority / stance:** Rigorous German **historical-philological** scholarship; myth defined broadly as "objectification of theological statements." Deep per-region, but skewed to the literate Old World; the Americas, Sub-Saharan Africa (beyond Berber), and Oceania are largely absent because the project stalled in Division I.

### 11. Yuri Berezkin — Electronic Analytic Catalogue of Folklore and Mythology Motifs

- **Creator:** Yuri E. Berezkin (anthropologist/folklorist, Kunstkamera / St. Petersburg).
- **Scale:** **~50,000 abstracts of oral texts worldwide**; parsed from ~6,239 books/articles by ~4,041 authors in 32 languages; **~2,564 motifs** coded and linked to **~958 ethnolinguistic groups**; distribution of ~2,000 cosmological/etiological/trickster motifs checked across ~1,000 traditions. https://www.mythologydatabase.com/ , https://sisu.ut.ee/wp-content/uploads/sites/172/berezkin_folklore_and_mythology_catalogue_its_lay-1_out_and_potential_for_research.pdf
- **Method / organizing principle:** **Motif-and-areal-distribution** database, not a narrative encyclopedia. A "motif" = an episode or image recurring in a community's narratives; motifs are mapped geographically to reconstruct deep-time cultural history and migrations (famously the **peopling of the New World** from folklore-motif distributions). Began with the Americas, then extended to the Old World. https://www.academia.edu/30094160/ , http://mundastudies.info/berezkin/
- **Authority / stance:** Highly regarded quantitative/statistical-cartographic approach; the largest single-scholar comparative motif dataset. Its "carving" of the world is by ethnolinguistic group and geographic area rather than literate/oral prestige — a deliberate counter to the literate-centric encyclopedias.

### 12. Aarne–Thompson–Uther (ATU) Index & Thompson's Motif-Index

- **ATU Index — *The Types of International Folktales*:** Antti Aarne (1910) → expanded/translated by Stith Thompson (1928, rev. 1961) → thoroughly revised by **Hans-Jörg Uther (2004), 3 volumes**, Folklore Fellows Communications (Finnish Academy of Science and Letters); updated 2024 edition. Classifies tale *types* into ~seven broad categories (e.g., Animal Tales; Tales of Magic 300–749; Religious Tales; Realistic/Novelle; Tales of the Stupid Ogre; Anecdotes/Jokes; Formula Tales). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarne%E2%80%93Thompson%E2%80%93Uther_Index , https://www.folklorefellows.fi/ffc-284-286-2024-edition/
- **Motif-Index of Folk-Literature (Stith Thompson):** **6 volumes** (1932–1936; rev. 1955–1958), a granular classification of narrative *motifs* (A cosmology/creation, B animals, ... through Z), cross-indexed with the tale-type index. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motif-Index_of_Folk-Literature , https://iupress.org/9780253338853/motif-index-of-folk-literature-volume-5/
- **Organizing principle:** **Numeric/alphabetic classification of narrative content** (types and motifs), transcending region — a comparative *tool*, not a mythography. The foundational infrastructure for comparative folklore (and the basis for Berezkin's and other digital catalogues). Historically Euro-centric in coverage, which Uther's revision worked to broaden.

### 13. ARAS — Archive for Research in Archetypal Symbolism

- **What it is:** An encyclopedic image archive of ~**18,000 photographic images** of art, ritual objects, and artifacts from all cultures and epochs (Paleolithic to modern), each with scholarly + archetypal commentary, cultural history, bibliography, and glossary. https://aras.org/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archive_for_Research_in_Archetypal_Symbolism
- **Organizing principle:** Cross-indexed by symbol/image; online as a subscription database (institutional members in NY, LA, Chicago, SF).
- **Authority / stance:** **Jungian / archetypal** — associated with the C. G. Jung Institutes; interprets images for their "transpersonal and symbolic" (archetypal) meaning across cultures. Valuable as a visual/symbolic reference; theoretically committed to a Jungian universalism that mainstream religious-studies scholarship treats cautiously. https://www.junginla.org/libraryaras/aras-archive-research-archetypal-symbolism

### 14. Theoi.com (Theoi Project)

- **Creator:** Aaron J. Atsma (Auckland, NZ), with University of Auckland and Leiden University library support; established 2000. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theoi_Project , https://wiki.digitalclassicist.org/Theoi_Project
- **Scale / scope:** Largest free web reference for **Greek mythology only**: **1,500+ pages, 1,200+ images**; a page per deity/creature with encyclopedia summary, extensive **primary-source quotations** (Greek and Roman texts), and ancient art. Also hosts a library of classical texts.
- **Organizing principle / stance:** Source-driven catalog of the Greek pantheon; explicitly **presents primary sources, not interpretation** ("analysis... beyond the scope of the project"). Reliable and heavily used, but single-tradition and non-analytical. https://www.theoi.com/

### 15. Database of Religious History (DRH)

- **What it is:** UBC-based (director **Edward Slingerland**), launched 2012 — "the world's first comprehensive, online quantitative and qualitative encyclopedia of religious and social history." https://arc.ubc.ca/digital-humanities-social-sciences/people-projects/database-religious-history , https://hecc.ubc.ca/the-database-of-religious-history/
- **Scale:** **200+ religious groups/traditions**, contributions from ~150–500+ scholars/experts.
- **Method / organizing principle:** A **standardized expert "poll"** — questions about religious groups across time/space (mostly pre-modern), coded Yes/No with qualitative comment boxes and references, enabling comparative analysis and visualization. https://www.michael.muthukrishna.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Slingerland-Monroe-Muthukrishna-2023_The-Database-of-Religious-History.pdf
- **Authority / stance:** Peer-expert-sourced, cultural-evolution/quantitative comparative religion. Carves the world by *religious group/entity* (not region or pantheon), an approach designed for statistical analysis rather than narrative reading.

## Part II — Cross-Cutting Synthesis

### A. How the major works divide world mythology (top-level partitions, side by side)

| Work | Organizing unit | Top-level divisions | Granularity pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New Larousse** (Guirand) | Region/culture essays | ~17–18: Prehistoric, Egyptian, Assyro-Babylonian, Phoenician, Greek, Roman, Celtic, Teutonic, Slavonic, Finno-Ugric, Persia, Indian, Chinese, Japanese, "Two Americas," Oceanic, "Black Africa" | Fine for literate Old World; whole New World / Oceania / Africa each one chapter |
| **Mythology of All Races** | One+ region per volume | 12 topical vols: Greek-Roman; Eddic; Celtic+Slavic; Finno-Ugric+Siberian; Semitic; Indian+Iranian; Armenian+African; Chinese+Japanese; Oceanic; N. American; Latin American; Egyptian+Indo-Chinese | Same skew: N. & Latin America = 2 vols total; Africa shares a vol; Oceania one vol |
| **Мифы народов мира** (Tokarev) | A–Z + synthetic per-people articles | Alphabetical, but with large survey entries for every people/region incl. Siberian, Caucasian, Paleo-Asiatic, Australian, African peoples | Most *even* coverage; strong on Eurasian/Siberian/oral traditions |
| **Bonnefoy / *Mythologies*** (Chicago) | Problem-essays, regrouped by region | Vol 1: Africa; Ancient Near East; Celts-Norse-Slavs-Caucasians; Greece; Rome. Vol 2: Christian-era West; South Asia/Iran/Buddhism; SE Asia; East & Inner Asia; Americas & South Pacific | Finer than Larousse in Asia; still pools "Americas & South Pacific" |
| **Haussig, Wörterbuch der Mythologie** | Regional volumes (Div. I only) | Ancient Near East; Old Europe; (Greek-Roman never issued); Caucasian & Iranian; Indian subcontinent; East Asia; Central Asia & N. Eurasia | Old-World-only; **no** Americas / Sub-Saharan Africa / Pacific (project stalled) |
| **Cotterell, *Dict. of World Mythology*** (OUP) | Region-grouped A–Z | 7: West Asia; South & Central Asia; East Asia; Europe; America; Africa; Oceania | Coarse continental buckets |
| **Encyclopedia of Religion** (Eliade/Jones) | A–Z, cross-cultural | No regional top level — alphabetical entries on traditions, deities, concepts, methods worldwide | Even by intent; comparative-religion frame |
| **Berezkin catalogue** | Ethnolinguistic group × motif | ~958 groups grouped by geographic/areal zones worldwide | Deliberately fine and even for *oral* traditions |
| **DRH** | Religious group/entity | 200+ groups, no regional hierarchy | Analysis-driven, group-level |

**The recurring pattern:** the print encyclopedias (Larousse, Mythology of All Races, Cotterell, Haussig, and to a lesser degree Bonnefoy) **split the literate Old World finely** — Greek, Roman, Egyptian, Mesopotamian/Semitic, Persian/Iranian, Indian, Chinese, Japanese each get their own section — but **pool oral / non-literate regions coarsely**: "the Two Americas / the Americas," "Oceania / the South Pacific," and "(Black/Sub-Saharan) Africa" repeatedly appear as single undivided units, despite each containing hundreds of distinct traditions.

### B. What earns a tradition its own section vs. pooling?

The decisive factor is **the volume of documented, written textual material**, not the number of ethnic groups or the intrinsic richness of the mythology:

- Traditions with **large surviving written corpora** (Homer/Hesiod for Greece, the Egyptian and Mesopotamian texts, the Vedas and Sanskrit epics, Chinese and Japanese classics, the Norse Eddas) are split finely — each gets a chapter, volume, or region because there is a canonical literature to expound. Theoi.com's very existence (an entire large site for Greece alone) is the extreme case.
- Traditions transmitted **orally** and documented mainly through ethnography — Sub-Saharan Africa, the Indigenous Americas, Oceania, Siberia/Central Asia — are pooled, even though they encompass far *more* distinct ethnic groups and languages than the literate blocks. "Black Africa" (Larousse) or "the Americas and the South Pacific" (Bonnefoy) collapse thousands of traditions into one heading.
- This is a **documentary/philological bias**, not a demographic one: sections track the density of *written sources and prior scholarship*, so literate civilizations with deep textual and academic traditions get disproportionate space.
- The notable **counter-examples** correct for this by changing the organizing unit: **Мифы народов мира** (built by the Moscow-Tartu semioticians) gives serious, even coverage to Siberian, Caucasian, Paleo-Asiatic, Australian, and African peoples; and **Berezkin's catalogue** and the **ATU/Thompson** apparatus abandon the pantheon/text unit entirely for the *motif* and the *ethnolinguistic group*, which lets oral traditions register at the same resolution as literate ones. The DRH similarly uses the *religious group* as its atom.

### C. Alphabetical-dictionary vs. region-essay organization — tradeoffs

**Alphabetical A–Z** (Encyclopedia of Religion, Funk & Wagnalls/Leach, Мифы народов мира, Man Myth & Magic, Lurker/Routledge, original French Bonnefoy):
- *Strengths:* fast look-up of a specific name/term; forces cross-cultural juxtaposition (Isis next to Ishtar); good for reference and comparison; scales to many small traditions without a prior taxonomy.
- *Weaknesses:* fragments each tradition into scattered entries; hard to read a coherent narrative of one mythology; presupposes the user already knows the name; foregrounds isolated figures over systems and structures.

**Region/culture essays** (New Larousse, Mythology of All Races, Haussig, Cotterell's grouped scheme, the restructured English *Mythologies*):
- *Strengths:* each tradition is presented as a coherent system with context and internal relations; better for learning a mythology whole; lets a specialist author give a synthetic account; good for narrative reading.
- *Weaknesses:* slow to find one figure; the section boundaries **encode and entrench the literate/oral bias** (you must decide up front how to carve the world, and coarse buckets result); cross-cultural comparison is harder; less flexible as new traditions are added.

**The hybrid that works best:** Мифы народов мира (A–Z *plus* large synthetic per-people articles) and *Mythologies* (essay-articles regrouped regionally for the English edition) show the two principles can be combined — quick entry access plus coherent regional synthesis. The purest analytic answer, though, is the **motif/database** model (Berezkin, ATU, Motif-Index, DRH): by indexing *content* rather than assuming a geographic table of contents, it sidesteps the "how to carve the world" problem altogether and treats oral and literate traditions with equal resolution — at the cost of narrative readability.

---

## Implications for mythoscope's section scheme

- The **literate/oral documentary bias is a feature of every region-organized reference**, not a quirk of Berezkin. A volume-balanced sectioning that splits the literate Old World and pools the oral world is therefore *consistent with the entire encyclopedia tradition* (Larousse, Mythology of All Races, Haussig, Bonnefoy), not an artifact.
- The two authorities that achieve **even coverage** — Мифы народов мира and Berezkin — do so by changing the atom (per-people synthetic articles; motif × ethnolinguistic group). That is the same move mythoscope makes with the motif matrix.
- A **hybrid presentation** (A–Z lookup + region-essay synthesis) is the empirically best-tested design; the motif-database layer is the analytic complement that keeps oral traditions at full resolution.
